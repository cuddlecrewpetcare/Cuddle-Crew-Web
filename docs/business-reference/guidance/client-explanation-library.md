# CLIENT EXPLANATION LIBRARY

## 1. Why does a visit cost this much?

### Approved Response

The rate covers the complete professional service—not only the minutes inside your home—including travel, scheduling, documentation, business and insurance costs, and responsibility for your pet and home.

### Intended Use

This explanation may be used for:

- pricing FAQs;
- service-page explanations;
- estimate-builder help text;
- Client messages;
- or other general pricing questions.

### Guardrail

Do not imply that Clients are directly billed separately for every internal business expense listed in this explanation.

The purpose is to explain what is reflected in professional Service pricing overall.

## 2. Why can't you come every other day for my cat?

### Approved Response

Because no one else will be home, I recommend at least daily in-person care so I can check food, water, litter, behavior, your cat's wellbeing, and the home—not just refill a feeder.

### Intended Use

Use when a Client asks why Cuddle Crew recommends daily in-person care rather than every-other-day vacation care for a cat who would otherwise be alone.

### Guardrail

Do not present daily care as an arbitrary pricing rule.

The explanation should remain grounded in welfare and meaningful in-person observation.

Current care standards generally recommend at least daily in-person care for a healthy adult cat while the owner is completely away, with individual adjustment for feeding, social needs, litter, medication, health, and routine.

## 3. Why do I need three dog visits?

### Approved Response

When a dog is completely alone, I look at the full routine—potty opportunities, meals, water, exercise, behavior, and how long they'll be without a person. For many adult dogs, about three care opportunities a day is a reasonable starting point, then I adjust to the individual dog.

### Intended Use

Use when explaining vacation-care frequency for an adult dog whose owner will be completely away.

### Guardrail

Do not convert “about three care opportunities a day” into a universal fixed rule for every dog.

Care frequency should be individualized based on factors such as:

- established routine;
- potty tolerance;
- meals;
- exercise;
- anxiety;
- age;
- health;
- medication;
- and how long the dog can safely and reasonably be alone.

Puppies, seniors, medical cases, anxious dogs, and other non-routine cases may require more frequent or individualized care.

## 4. Why an additional-pet fee?

### Approved Response

It reflects the additional care and responsibility when everything still fits the booked service. If the routine needs more time, I'll recommend a longer visit instead.

### Intended Use

Use for:

- pricing FAQs;
- estimate-builder explanations;
- additional-pet line-item help text;
- Client quote discussions.

### Guardrail

An additional-pet fee does not create unlimited capacity within a Service.

If the complete routine does not realistically fit the booked duration:

- recommend a longer Service;
- recommend additional Services;
- or require personalized review.

Do not use an additional-pet fee as a substitute for adequate time.

Grouped-animal arrangements such as aquariums, colonies, flocks, or communal enclosures may require review based on actual husbandry workload rather than mechanically applying a fee to every individual animal.

## 8. Why isn't midday included with Overnight Care?

### Approved Response

The Overnight covers the agreed evening-through-morning window. The standard midday add-on is a separate 30-minute daytime appointment. Longer midday care uses the normal 60- or 90-minute Service rate, and a midday walk uses the appropriate Dog Walk rate.

### Intended Use

Use for:

- Overnight FAQ;
- Service comparison;
- pricing page;
- estimate builder;
- Client quote explanation.

### Guardrail

Standard Overnight Care generally covers approximately 6:00 PM through 8:00 AM and is not 24-hour continuous care.

The standard midday add-on means **one separate 30-minute daytime care visit**.

Do not describe the midday add-on as:

- unlimited daytime care;
- continuous presence;
- or care automatically included in every Overnight.

## 9. Can you squeeze it into 30 minutes?

### Approved Response

I want to complete the routine safely without rushing. If it doesn't realistically fit 30 minutes, I'll recommend the Service length that does.

### Intended Use

## 11. Why do you recommend daily care for rabbits or other small animals?

### Approved Response

Even when food and water can be prepared ahead of time, I still want to check the pet in person. Appetite, water intake, stool, enclosure condition, behavior, and overall wellbeing can change quickly, so regular in-person care is an important part of the care plan.

### Intended Use

Use as a general client-friendly welfare explanation for small-animal vacation care.

### Guardrail

Current care standards generally recommend:

- rabbits: at least daily, often 1–2 times per day;
- guinea pigs: at least daily, sometimes 1–2 times per day;
- other small mammals: generally a daily in-person check.

Actual frequency remains individualized.

Do not present one schedule as universally appropriate for all species.

## 12. Why can't an automatic feeder or camera replace a visit?

### Approved Response

Those tools can be helpful support, but they can't fully replace an in-person welfare check. A visit lets me see the pet directly, confirm food and water, check elimination and behavior, notice changes in the home, and respond if something isn't working as expected.

### Intended Use

Use for:

- vacation-care FAQ;
- care-frequency explanations;
- estimate-builder guidance;
- Client conversations about reducing visits.

### Guardrail

Do not criticize Clients for using:

- automatic feeders;
- fountains;
- cameras;
- self-cleaning litter boxes;
- or other care technology.

These devices may support a care plan.

They should not automatically replace the in-person care frequency Cuddle Crew considers adequate.

## 13. Why can't I just choose the cheapest visit length?

### Approved Response

I’ll always try to recommend the most efficient option that still gives me enough time to complete the full routine safely and thoroughly. If everything fits in the shorter visit, great. If it doesn’t, I’ll recommend the duration that actually fits the care your pets need.

### Intended Use

Use when explaining duration recommendations without sounding punitive or upsell-focused.

### Guardrail

The recommendation should be based on actual required care time, not maximizing revenue.

Use the shortest Service that reasonably fits the complete routine.

Do not undersell required time solely to win a booking.

## 14. Why do you use arrival windows instead of exact appointment times?

### Approved Response

Arrival windows give me enough flexibility to provide each pet their full care without rushing if traffic, a prior pet’s needs, or another unexpected delay comes up. If your pet has a genuinely time-sensitive need, let me know so I can review whether an exact-time visit is possible.

### Intended Use

Use for:

- scheduling FAQ;
- booking form help text;
- Client onboarding;
- Service-window explanation.

### Guardrail

Routine daytime appointments use Service windows rather than exact-minute guarantees.

Current standard windows are generally:

- 9 AM–12 PM
- 12 PM–3 PM
- 3 PM–6 PM
- 6 PM–9 PM

Exact-time/high-risk medication or other truly time-critical care requires prior review.

Do not promise exact-minute arrival merely to secure a booking.

## 19. Why might you recommend a longer visit instead of charging a special fee?

### Approved Response

If the main issue is that the routine simply takes more time, I’d rather recommend enough actual care time than add a random fee. That way, you’re paying for the Service time your pets genuinely need.

### Intended Use

Use when explaining why Cuddle Crew generally handles time-intensive care through the duration ladder rather than miscellaneous surcharges.

### Guardrail

This explanation reflects the internal pricing principle:

**More time → sell more appropriate Service time.**

Do not use it to imply that all complexity can be solved with more time.

Where the issue is:

- safety;
- advanced medical scope;
- behavior risk;
- insurance;
- unusual husbandry;
- or another scope concern,

use personalized review rather than simply increasing duration.

## 20. Why don't you just charge extra for a difficult or risky situation?

### Approved Response

If something changes the safety or scope of the care, I’d rather review whether I’m the right person to provide it than charge an arbitrary “risk fee.” Sometimes the right answer is a different care plan, a longer visit, or a provider with more specialized experience.

### Intended Use

Use sparingly where a Client directly asks about behavior, medical, species, or other complexity-based pricing.

### Guardrail

Do not publicly characterize a particular Client or pet as “risky,” “difficult,” or “high risk.”

Do not expose internal behavior or medical-review classifications.

The core business rule is that more risk, medical complexity, behavior difficulty, unfamiliar husbandry, or unsafe conditions should trigger:

- review;
- modification;
- referral;
- or decline

rather than an arbitrary surcharge.

## General Client-Explanation Principles

Client-facing explanations should be:

- warm;
- clear;
- concise;
- non-defensive;
- specific;
- and grounded in actual Cuddle Crew policy.

Prefer explaining the **reason behind the rule** rather than simply repeating the rule.

For example:

Instead of:

> “We require daily visits.”

Prefer:

> “I recommend at least daily in-person care so I can directly check food, water, litter, behavior, wellbeing, and the home.”

## Explain Without Overexplaining

FAQ answers should generally give the Client enough information to understand the policy without exposing unnecessary internal operations.

Do not expose:

- private capacity calculations;
- route order;
- other Client bookings;
- private home/service-area origin;
- internal risk scores;
- behavior severity classifications;
- insurance-analysis notes;
- decline rubrics;
- or sitter personal schedule.

## Policy Controls Over Explanation

This library is explanatory guidance.

Where the exact rule matters, defer to the appropriate CURRENT / APPROVED authoritative source.

Examples:

- rates → Pricing, Fees & Surcharge Policy;
- cancellation → Cancellation, Booking Change & Refund Policy;
- exact holiday periods → Holiday / Peak-Date Calendar;
- booking acceptance → Booking Acceptance / Risk Triage;
- medication → Medication Scope Review and applicable Client-facing consent;
- behavior → Behavior Risk Review and applicable Client-facing agreement;
- Overnight requirements → Overnight Acceptance and applicable Client-facing addendum.

Do not use FAQ wording to silently change the underlying business rule.

## Tone

Approved explanations should sound like Cuddle Crew, not like a legal contract.

Prefer language such as:

- “I recommend…”
- “I want to make sure…”
- “That helps me…”
- “If the routine needs more time…”
- “I’ll review…”
- “I can accommodate…”

Avoid unnecessarily harsh wording such as:

- “You are required to…”
- “Absolutely not…”
- “Your pet is high-risk…”
- “Your request is unreasonable…”
- “We charge you because…”

unless a stricter statement is genuinely necessary and supported by an authoritative Client-facing policy.
