## Purpose

This document defines the internal booking-acceptance and risk-triage process used by Cuddle Crew Pet Care LLC.

It is used to determine whether a requested booking:

- can be accepted as requested;
- should be accepted with conditions;
- requires a modified Service plan;
- requires personalized review;
- should be referred to another provider;
- should be declined;
- or should remain pending until missing information or requirements are resolved.

This document is an internal operational reference.

Its purpose is not to assign a public "risk score" to Clients or pets. It exists to prevent automation, pricing, payment, or scheduling convenience from overriding safety, animal welfare, training, insurance scope, realistic Service duration, route capacity, access readiness, or other material booking requirements.

The public website may use this logic to decide when automation should stop and human review should begin, but it should not expose the complete internal triage rubric, thresholds, decision reasoning, or decline criteria.

Precise Petcare should remain the operational source of truth for current Client, pet, care, access, schedule, and confirmed booking records.

## Authoritative Content

# BOOKING ACCEPTANCE / RISK TRIAGE

Before accepting a booking, review each applicable category below.

## 1. Safety

- [ ] Can Cuddle Crew safely provide the requested care?
- [ ] Can the required handling be completed without unreasonable risk to the pet, sitter, Client, third parties, or property?
- [ ] Can entry, exit, confinement, walking, feeding, medication, separation, and other required tasks be performed safely?
- [ ] Are known behavior, escape, aggression, equipment, environmental, or home-safety concerns reasonably manageable?

If safe care cannot reasonably be provided, do not accept the booking as requested.

Possible outcomes include:

- modify the Service plan;
- require additional information;
- require a behavior/safety review;
- use an approved safer alternative;
- refer out;
- or decline.

Payment or willingness to pay a higher price does not make an unsafe Service acceptable.

## 2. Training, Service, Legal, and Insurance Scope

- [ ] Is the requested care within Cuddle Crew’s current training and competence?
- [ ] Is the requested care within the approved Service scope?
- [ ] Is the requested care compatible with current insurance coverage?
- [ ] Is the requested activity legally and operationally appropriate?
- [ ] Does the booking avoid requiring procedures, transportation, handling, or services outside approved scope?

Services outside training, insurance, safety, legal, or agreed scope may be modified, declined, or referred out.

A higher price must not be used to make an otherwise out-of-scope Service acceptable.

## 3. Care Frequency and Animal Welfare

- [ ] Is the requested care frequency adequate while the Client is away?
- [ ] Are feeding, water, elimination, medication, companionship, monitoring, and other welfare needs reasonably covered?
- [ ] Is the gap between responsible in-person care appropriate for the specific pet and circumstances?
- [ ] Have age, medical condition, routine, anxiety, mobility, continence, housetraining, species, and other relevant factors been considered?

Automated feeders, water fountains, cameras, self-cleaning litter boxes, or similar equipment may support a care plan but do not automatically replace appropriate in-person welfare checks.

If the requested care frequency is inadequate for animal welfare:

- recommend an adjusted plan;
- require additional visits;
- require a different Service structure;
- or decline the booking.

Do not approve an inadequate care plan merely because it is cheaper.

## 4. Service Duration Fit

- [ ] Does the complete care routine realistically fit within the requested duration?
- [ ] Can all required pets, enclosures, feeding, water, medication, elimination, walking, enrichment, cleanup, separation, home tasks, and documentation reasonably fit?
- [ ] Does the routine require 30, 60, or 90 minutes?
- [ ] Does the routine require multiple Services?
- [ ] Does the routine require Overnight Care plus separate daytime care?
- [ ] Does the requested scope exceed the standard daytime structure?

General duration guidance:

### 30 Minutes

Usually appropriate when routine care such as feeding, water, potty/litter/enclosure care, straightforward medication, brief enrichment, and documentation comfortably fit.

### 60 Minutes

May be more appropriate for multiple pets or enclosures, longer walking or enrichment, slower senior care, complex food preparation, several care tasks, or more involved routine cleanup.

### 90 Minutes

May be more appropriate for extended routines, longer companionship/enrichment, multiple pets/enclosures, slower or more involved care, or a walk plus additional home-care tasks requiring the extra time.

### Personalized Review

Use personalized review when:

- even 90 minutes may not realistically fit;
- behavior or medical needs materially change scope;
- separation or rotation requirements are complex;
- unusual species or husbandry are involved;
- continuous care is requested;
- or another unusual condition materially changes the standard Service structure.

Do not shorten the Service below what the care routine reasonably requires solely to reduce price.

## 5. Pet and Species Disclosure

- [ ] Are all pets in the household disclosed?
- [ ] Are all pets receiving care profiled?
- [ ] Are all relevant species identified?
- [ ] Are all enclosures, tanks, cages, colonies, flocks, or communal setups disclosed?
- [ ] Are separate routines or materially different husbandry needs understood?

Undisclosed pets or materially different care responsibilities require review before acceptance.

Grouped-animal setups should be evaluated according to actual husbandry workload, separate care routines, and Service fit rather than automatically treating every individual animal as identical workload.

## 6. Behavior and Handling

- [ ] Are bite or snap history and known triggers disclosed?
- [ ] Are guarding issues disclosed?
- [ ] Are leash reactivity and pulling understood?
- [ ] Are escape or door-dashing risks understood?
- [ ] Are stranger fear or handling sensitivities understood?
- [ ] Are pet-to-pet conflict and separation requirements workable?
- [ ] Are predator/prey management requirements workable?
- [ ] Is required handling equipment appropriate and safe?
- [ ] Can required handling be completed without escalating unsafe restraint?

Significant behavior or safety concerns require personalized review.

Known serious bite history or materially elevated escape/aggression risk may require:

- modified care;
- additional safety conditions;
- referral;
- or decline.

A dog that cannot safely be leashed must not be forced into a walk.

Medication must not be forced through escalating unsafe restraint.

## 7. Medication and Medical Care

- [ ] Are medication details complete?
- [ ] Is the administration method understood?
- [ ] Is timing workable?
- [ ] Is the medication within approved scope?
- [ ] Can it reasonably be completed within the booked Service?
- [ ] Does it require forceful restraint?
- [ ] Does it involve injection, exact-time administration, advanced medical technique, or other elevated complexity?
- [ ] Is the medication plan realistically compatible with available scheduling?

Routine agreed oral or topical medication may be accepted where it falls within approved scope and fits the booked Service.

Advanced, high-risk, exact-time, unusually time-intensive, restraint-intensive, or otherwise complex medication requires scope review.

Possible outcomes include:

- accept;
- accept with conditions;
- require longer Service;
- modify schedule;
- refer out;
- or decline.

Do not create an arbitrary "medical risk surcharge" merely because a medication case is more complex.

More time may justify more booked time.

More risk or scope complexity requires review, not a random fee.

## 8. Separation and Multi-Pet Management

- [ ] Are feeding separations understood?
- [ ] Are pet-to-pet conflicts understood?
- [ ] Are predator/prey separation requirements workable?
- [ ] Can door, gate, crate, room, enclosure, or rotation requirements be completed safely?
- [ ] Does the rotation process realistically fit the booked duration?
- [ ] Is there a clear plan if one pet cannot be safely moved or handled?

Complex separation or rotation requirements may require:

- a longer Service;
- an adjusted routine;
- additional Service;
- or personalized review.

Do not assume an additional-pet fee alone makes a complex multi-pet routine workable.

## 9. Access and Home Safety

- [ ] Is primary access reliable?
- [ ] Is backup access available where appropriate?
- [ ] Have keys, codes, gates, locks, alarms, or building restrictions been tested or adequately verified?
- [ ] Are parking and entry logistics workable?
- [ ] Are expected occupants or visitors disclosed?
- [ ] Are cameras/audio devices appropriately disclosed?
- [ ] Does the home appear reasonably safe to enter and perform the requested Services?

Unresolved access problems prevent automatic booking approval.

If the location, occupants, access system, or other circumstances reasonably appear unsafe, Cuddle Crew may decline entry, modify care, or decline the booking.

## 10. Travel Zone and Route Feasibility

- [ ] Has the applicable travel zone been confirmed?
- [ ] Is the one-way travel burden operationally workable?
- [ ] Is the booking financially reasonable under the current pricing structure?
- [ ] Can the route be incorporated without compromising other confirmed Client care?
- [ ] Is the Service location within the normal accepted travel structure?
- [ ] If outside the normal travel structure, has personalized review been completed?

Current daytime travel structure:

- Core: approximately 0–10 minutes one way
- Standard: more than 10–20 minutes one way
- Extended: more than 20–30 minutes one way
- Far Extended: more than 30–45 minutes one way
- Beyond 45 minutes: personalized review / generally unavailable unless specifically approved

Travel classification should ordinarily use typical one-way travel time under normal driving conditions rather than temporary traffic.

Overnight Services outside the Standard Zone require individualized review.

A travel fee does not guarantee acceptance.

## 11. Route and Time-Block Capacity

- [ ] Is there enough capacity within the relevant Service window?
- [ ] Has ordinary drive time been considered?
- [ ] Has booked hands-on Service time been considered?
- [ ] Has journal/documentation time been considered?
- [ ] Has a reasonable operational buffer been considered?
- [ ] Can the booking be added without creating unreliable or unsafe scheduling?
- [ ] Can existing confirmed Clients still be served appropriately?

Capacity must account for more than the booked Service duration alone.

Do not treat a technically open calendar slot as automatically available if route, documentation, travel, or buffer requirements make the booking impractical.

Do not expose internal capacity calculations publicly.

## 12. Scheduling Requirements

- [ ] Are requested Service windows workable?
- [ ] Are any exact-time requirements present?
- [ ] Is exact-time care necessary or merely preferred?
- [ ] Can time-sensitive feeding, medication, elimination, or other care be reliably accommodated?
- [ ] Does the requested schedule conflict with existing commitments?

Routine daytime care should generally use approved Service windows rather than exact-minute guarantees.

Exact-time care requires personalized review unless it has already been specifically approved.

## 13. Holiday and Peak-Date Capacity

- [ ] Does the booking fall on an approved holiday or peak-date period?
- [ ] Is holiday/peak pricing applicable?
- [ ] Are holiday cancellation terms applicable?
- [ ] Is sufficient holiday capacity available?
- [ ] Have applicable holiday conditions been disclosed before confirmation?

The existence of a holiday surcharge does not guarantee availability.

The approved Holiday / Peak-Date Calendar controls which dates qualify.

Do not invent holiday dates.

## 14. Short-Notice and Same-Day Requests

- [ ] Is the request less than 24 hours before daytime Service?
- [ ] Is it a same-calendar-day daytime request?
- [ ] Is the Overnight requested less than 48 hours in advance?
- [ ] Can the request safely and operationally be accommodated?
- [ ] Does accepting the request create unreasonable route, schedule, or administrative disruption?

Applicable current rules include:

- less than 24 hours before daytime Service, but not same calendar day: may trigger the approved short-notice fee;
- same-day daytime request: may trigger the approved same-day fee;
- same-day fee replaces the standard short-notice fee rather than stacking with it;
- Overnight requested less than 48 hours before Service may trigger the approved Overnight short-notice fee.

A short-notice fee does not guarantee acceptance.

## 15. Meet & Greet and Onboarding

- [ ] Complimentary Meet & Greet/onboarding has been completed.

**OR**

- [ ] A rare exception has been personally approved.

- [ ] Client intake information is complete.
- [ ] Pet profiles are complete.
- [ ] Relevant veterinary/emergency information is complete.
- [ ] Required authorizations are complete.
- [ ] Access has been tested.
- [ ] Required agreements are accepted.
- [ ] Required payment conditions are satisfied.

Booking acceptance should not bypass unresolved onboarding requirements merely because the Client wants to begin quickly.

## 16. Shared Care and Third Parties

- [ ] Has the Client disclosed other caregivers or providers?
- [ ] Are responsibilities clearly divided?
- [ ] Are medication responsibilities clearly assigned?
- [ ] Are access arrangements workable for all providers?
- [ ] Is emergency responsibility understood?
- [ ] Is there a reliable plan rather than an assumption that another person "might stop by"?

Cuddle Crew does not silently assume unlimited duties if another caregiver fails to perform their assigned responsibilities.

Complex or unclear shared-care arrangements require review.

## 17. Overnight-Specific Review

For Overnight requests:

- [ ] Requested scope fits the standard Overnight framework.
- [ ] Client understands Overnight Care is generally approximately 6:00 PM–8:00 AM and is not 24-hour continuous care.
- [ ] Separate daytime needs have been identified.
- [ ] Home sleeping/bathroom/HVAC requirements are workable.
- [ ] Cameras/audio devices do not create unreasonable privacy concerns.
- [ ] After-dark access and parking are workable.
- [ ] Pet behavior, medication, and escape risk are workable overnight.
- [ ] Travel-zone impact is workable.
- [ ] Long-Stay Review has been completed if required.

Overnight Care outside the Standard travel zone requires individualized review.

## 18. Long-Stay Review

Where the booking consists of seven or more consecutive Overnights:

- [ ] Long-Stay Review completed.
- [ ] Personal schedule sustainability reviewed.
- [ ] Daytime and midday obligations reviewed.
- [ ] Client supplies are sufficient.
- [ ] Other Client commitments remain workable.
- [ ] Emergency contacts and backup pathways are current.
- [ ] Home tasks remain within reasonable scope.

A long stay does not automatically require a special risk surcharge.

The issue is sustainability, scope, capacity, and continuity.

## 19. Transportation

- [ ] Does the requested Service require pet transportation?
- [ ] Is transportation currently within approved insurance and business scope?
- [ ] Is an alternative authorized transport pathway available if needed?
- [ ] Has the request been separately reviewed?

Pet transportation is not automatically approved.

Transportation requests require personalized review.

## 20. Unusual Species or Husbandry

- [ ] Is the species within current competence and Service scope?
- [ ] Are species-specific feeding, temperature, habitat, enclosure, medication, and emergency requirements understood?
- [ ] Is care safely achievable within the booked duration?
- [ ] Is specialized husbandry required?
- [ ] Is referral more appropriate?

Birds, reptiles, fish, exotics, unusual small animals, and specialized husbandry may require personalized review.

Do not accept unfamiliar or unsupported husbandry solely because the task appears simple.

## 21. Continuous-Care Requests

- [ ] Does the Client expect continuous presence?
- [ ] Does the Client expect the sitter not to leave the home?
- [ ] Does the requested care materially exceed the normal Overnight or daytime Service structure?
- [ ] Is the requested scope operationally sustainable?

Continuous care is not part of the normal standard Overnight Service.

Continuous-care requests require personalized review and may be unavailable.

## 22. Pricing and Scope Confirmation

- [ ] Final Service type confirmed.
- [ ] Final duration confirmed.
- [ ] Final pet count and applicable additional-pet pricing confirmed.
- [ ] Applicable travel treatment confirmed.
- [ ] Applicable holiday or short-notice treatment confirmed.
- [ ] Any custom pricing reviewed.
- [ ] Final quoted price disclosed before confirmation.
- [ ] Booking-specific reservation-payment requirement disclosed where applicable.
- [ ] Applicable payment deadline established.

Pricing can only be finalized after scope is understood.

A mathematically calculable price does not automatically mean the booking is acceptable.

## 23. Payment

- [ ] Applicable payment requirement has been satisfied.
- [ ] Any reservation payment due at confirmation has been satisfied.
- [ ] Any approved alternative payment arrangement is documented.

Payment does not override:

- safety;
- welfare;
- Service scope;
- insurance limitations;
- route feasibility;
- scheduling capacity;
- access readiness;
- or unresolved review requirements.

A paid booking can still require modification, referral, or cancellation where continued Service would otherwise be unsafe or outside scope, subject to applicable Client-facing policies.

## 24. Stop & Review Triggers

The following conditions require personalized review rather than automatic approval:

- 4 or more dogs;
- 5 or more total pets;
- 3 or more materially distinct care types or routines;
- advanced or high-risk medication;
- exact-time care;
- significant behavior or safety concern;
- complex predator/prey separation;
- unusual species;
- continuous-care request;
- pet transportation;
- more than 45 minutes typical one-way travel;
- complex or extended-area Overnight Care;
- routine that may not fit within 90 minutes;
- or another material circumstance that does not fit the standard Service structure.

These are **review triggers**, not automatic decline rules.

A review may result in:

- acceptance as requested;
- acceptance with conditions;
- longer Service;
- additional Service;
- revised care plan;
- custom pricing;
- referral;
- decline;
- or another reasonable outcome consistent with current policy and scope.

## 25. Booking Decision

Select one:

- [ ] **ACCEPT**
- [ ] **ACCEPT WITH CONDITIONS**
- [ ] **MODIFY**
- [ ] **PERSONALIZED REVIEW REQUIRED**
- [ ] **REFER**
- [ ] **DECLINE**
- [ ] **PENDING**

### Conditions / Required Changes

Record internally:

- required Service duration;
- required visit frequency;
- required access changes;
- required equipment;
- required medication conditions;
- required separation plan;
- required forms or authorizations;
- required payment or booking conditions;
- required scheduling conditions;
- or other relevant approval conditions.

### Reason / Internal Notes

Record only information reasonably necessary for:

- safety;
- care planning;
- scope;
- scheduling;
- documentation;
- or future booking consistency.

Avoid irrelevant subjective commentary about the Client or household.

## 26. Acceptance Principle

A booking should be accepted only when Cuddle Crew can reasonably provide the requested care:

- safely;
- reliably;
- within training and insurance scope;
- at an adequate care frequency;
- within a realistic Service duration;
- with workable access;
- within available route and schedule capacity;
- and under a clearly confirmed Service plan.

Pricing is applied after scope is understood.

**More time should generally be addressed with more booked time.**

**More risk, scope complexity, or uncertainty should generally trigger review rather than an arbitrary surcharge.**

A higher price does not make unsafe, unlawful, uninsurable, or out-of-scope care acceptable.

## Website Implementation Notes

This document is the primary internal reference for deciding when automated booking or estimate logic must stop and require human review.

The full triage rubric must not be publicly exposed.

### Public Website Goal

The public website should help Clients:

- understand available Services;
- estimate likely pricing;
- provide preliminary information;
- identify obvious Service-fit issues;
- request care;
- and understand when personalized review is needed.

It should not attempt to publicly reproduce the internal acceptance process.

### Automated Acceptance vs. Review

Website automation may proceed normally when:

- the requested Service fits the standard Service structure;
- required information is available;
- no review trigger is detected;
- pricing can be determined from current authoritative rules;
- and no safety, scope, access, capacity, or unusual-care concern is apparent.

When one or more review conditions are detected, the website should stop automatic finalization and return a client-facing result such as:

**Personalized review required**

or:

**We’ll review your care plan before confirming your booking.**

Do not expose the internal trigger or threshold unless that specific information has been explicitly approved for public display.

### Review Trigger Privacy

Do not publicly display the complete internal list of:

- pet-count thresholds;
- care-type thresholds;
- behavior-risk triggers;
- medication-risk categories;
- route/capacity limits;
- transportation conditions;
- scope limitations;
- or other internal acceptance logic.

Public pages may explain more generally that some bookings require individual review because of:

- household size;
- care complexity;
- medical needs;
- behavior or safety;
- unusual species;
- travel;
- scheduling;
- or nonstandard Service needs.

### Estimator Behavior

The estimate builder may calculate a preliminary price only where the standard pricing structure clearly applies.

The estimator must not assume that:

- a calculable price means the booking is acceptable;
- an additional-pet fee means the complete routine fits;
- a travel fee guarantees acceptance;
- a short-notice fee guarantees availability;
- an Overnight base rate applies to every household;
- a higher price resolves safety or scope concerns;
- or a Client-selected duration is automatically adequate.

Where standard pricing can be calculated but a review trigger is present, the website may show an estimate while also stating:

**Personalized review required before booking confirmation.**

### Pet Counts

Do not publicly expose the internal automatic-review thresholds merely because they are used in website logic.

If a household exceeds automatic standard logic, route the request to personalized review.

### Care-Type Complexity

Do not expose the internal count used to determine materially distinct care routines.

Public UX may instead say:

**This care plan includes multiple different routines and needs a personalized review.**

### Behavior

Website forms may collect behavior disclosures necessary for safe care.

Do not show the Client:

- a risk score;
- internal severity rating;
- acceptance probability;
- sitter-safety classification;
- or internal decline reasoning.

Use general statuses such as:

- Information received
- Under review
- Additional information needed
- Personalized review required

### Medication

Website forms may collect preliminary medication information.

Do not automatically approve:

- injections;
- advanced medical procedures;
- exact-time medication;
- high-risk medication;
- medication requiring forceful restraint;
- or other complex medical care.

Route those conditions to human review.

### Travel

The website may use approved travel logic to determine ordinary daytime pricing.

Do not expose:

- the private service-area reference point;
- private origin coordinates;
- private routing assumptions;
- API secrets;
- or internal route-capacity data.

Travel beyond the standard automatic range should route to personalized review.

### Route and Capacity

Website availability logic must not treat an open-looking time slot as automatic capacity.

Internal capacity may include:

- Service duration;
- drive time;
- journal/documentation time;
- buffer;
- existing Clients;
- route order;
- and other operational constraints.

Do not expose those calculations publicly.

### Booking Status

Recommended public/client-facing states may include:

- Request received
- Additional information needed
- Under review
- Personalized review required
- Booking confirmed
- Unable to accommodate

Do not expose internal decision labels such as:

- high risk;
- route failure;
- poor fit;
- decline threshold;
- or capacity burden.

### Pricing

All pricing used by website logic must come from CURRENT / APPROVED pricing references.

This risk-triage document does not create new rates or surcharges.

It determines whether:

- standard pricing may be used;
- Service scope must change;
- or personalized review is required.

### Safety Override

No website button, payment state, quote status, or Client preference should automatically override a safety or scope stop.

If the logic detects a material safety or scope issue, automated booking finalization should stop.

### Precise Petcare Handoff

Final Client-specific booking details should be reflected in Precise Petcare.

Website information should be treated as preliminary until confirmed through the approved booking workflow.

Do not create a second authoritative care-plan database on the website.

### Conflict Handling

If website logic conflicts with this CURRENT / APPROVED triage reference:

1. flag the conflict;
2. identify the relevant feature or code path;
3. preserve the authoritative internal rule;
4. do not weaken safety or scope logic simply to keep automation running.

If this document conflicts with a CURRENT / APPROVED Client-facing agreement or policy, the Client-facing agreement or policy controls the contractual subject and the conflict should be flagged for human review.
