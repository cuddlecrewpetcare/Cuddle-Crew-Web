## Purpose

This document defines the internal suitability review for Cuddle Crew Pet Care LLC’s 90-Minute Adventure Walk Service.

The goal is to determine whether an extended walk is a safe and appropriate Service for the individual dog based on the dog’s normal activity level, age, conditioning, mobility, respiratory considerations, behavior, leash handling, paw/surface conditions, weather, route conditions, and other relevant safety factors.

This document is an internal operational reference.

The public website may explain that Adventure Walks are intended for dogs suited to longer-duration activity and are subject to safety and suitability review, but it should not expose the full internal evaluation process, medical-style screening criteria, or private decision notes.

Cuddle Crew does not diagnose medical conditions, prescribe exercise, or replace veterinary guidance.

## Authoritative Content

# 90-MINUTE ADVENTURE WALK SUITABILITY REVIEW

Before accepting or performing a 90-Minute Adventure Walk, review the applicable factors below.

## 1. Normal Activity and Conditioning

- [ ] Dog is accustomed to longer-duration walking or comparable activity.
- [ ] Client-provided history supports that the dog normally tolerates extended activity.
- [ ] The requested 90-minute duration is reasonably consistent with the dog’s established routine or conditioning.
- [ ] There is no known recent significant reduction in normal activity that would make an extended outing questionable.

A 90-Minute Adventure Walk should not be treated as a way to suddenly increase a sedentary or poorly conditioned dog’s exercise load.

Where the dog is not accustomed to extended activity, consider:

- recommending a 60-minute walk;
- recommending a 30-minute walk;
- gradually building duration through the Client’s own normal routine;
- or requiring personalized review.

## 2. Age and Life Stage

- [ ] Dog’s age or developmental stage has been reviewed.
- [ ] Puppy-specific exercise and development considerations have been identified where relevant.
- [ ] Senior-dog mobility, endurance, comfort, continence, and recovery considerations have been reviewed where relevant.
- [ ] Age alone is not being used as an automatic acceptance or decline rule.

Age should be considered together with the individual dog’s normal activity, health history provided by the Client, mobility, comfort, and conditioning.

Do not assume that all young dogs can safely tolerate long-duration exercise.

Do not assume that all senior dogs are unsuitable.

## 3. Mobility, Injury, and Recovery Status

- [ ] Current mobility appears consistent with an extended walk based on Client-provided information and normal observation.
- [ ] Known orthopedic conditions have been disclosed.
- [ ] Known injuries have been disclosed.
- [ ] Recent surgery or recovery status has been disclosed.
- [ ] Limping, stiffness, pain behavior, weakness, or reduced mobility concerns have been reviewed.
- [ ] Any Client-provided activity restrictions have been documented.

If current mobility or recovery status creates uncertainty about whether a 90-minute walk is appropriate, do not automatically proceed with the extended walk.

Possible outcomes include:

- shorter walk;
- lower-intensity care;
- modified route;
- personalized review;
- or referral back to the Client/veterinarian for appropriate activity guidance.

## 4. Respiratory and Heat-Sensitivity Considerations

- [ ] Brachycephalic characteristics have been considered where relevant.
- [ ] Known respiratory concerns disclosed by the Client have been reviewed.
- [ ] Known heat intolerance has been reviewed.
- [ ] Known exercise intolerance has been reviewed.
- [ ] The dog’s normal breathing and recovery pattern during ordinary walks is understood where possible.

Dogs with elevated heat or respiratory sensitivity may require:

- shorter duration;
- slower pace;
- more rest;
- more conservative weather limits;
- modified route;
- or decline of the Adventure Walk on a given day.

Cuddle Crew does not diagnose respiratory disease or determine veterinary exercise prescriptions.

## 5. Leash Behavior and Handling

- [ ] Dog can be safely leashed.
- [ ] Normal leash equipment is secure and appropriate.
- [ ] Dog’s pulling level is manageable for extended duration.
- [ ] Leash reactivity is understood.
- [ ] Fear, freezing, bolting, or escape behavior is understood.
- [ ] The dog can be safely managed around expected environmental triggers.
- [ ] Required handling does not rely on forceful or unsafe restraint.

An Adventure Walk should not be accepted where the dog cannot be safely controlled for the expected outing.

Possible alternatives include:

- shorter walk;
- lower-stimulation route;
- yard/potty care;
- enrichment at home;
- behavior/safety review;
- referral;
- or decline.

## 6. Dog-to-Dog and Environmental Reactivity

- [ ] Dog’s response to unfamiliar dogs is understood.
- [ ] Dog’s response to people, bicycles, runners, vehicles, wildlife, noises, or other common outdoor stimuli is reasonably understood.
- [ ] Known triggers can be managed with safe distance and route choices.
- [ ] The expected route does not create an unreasonable likelihood of unavoidable trigger exposure.

Where reactivity materially increases risk over a longer outing, the Service should be modified or reviewed rather than assuming duration alone is appropriate.

## 7. Paw Condition and Surface Safety

- [ ] Paw condition has been considered where relevant.
- [ ] Known paw injuries or sensitivities have been disclosed.
- [ ] Nail or foot issues likely to affect walking have been considered.
- [ ] Planned surfaces are reasonably appropriate.
- [ ] Hot pavement risk has been considered.
- [ ] Ice, sharp debris, rough terrain, foxtails, or other local surface hazards have been considered where relevant.

The route or outdoor portion should be shortened, modified, or discontinued if surface conditions become unsafe.

## 8. Weather, Heat, Air Quality, and Storm Conditions

Before and during the Adventure Walk, consider:

- temperature;
- direct sun;
- shade availability;
- pavement/surface temperature;
- humidity;
- wildfire smoke;
- air quality;
- rain;
- lightning;
- wind;
- storms;
- flooding;
- and other relevant local hazards.

- [ ] Current conditions are suitable for the planned outing.
- [ ] Conditions are suitable for this individual dog.
- [ ] A safer modification is available if conditions change.

The planned outdoor portion may be:

- shortened;
- moved to a safer route;
- slowed;
- broken up with rest;
- replaced in part with indoor enrichment or companionship;
- or discontinued where needed for safety.

Safety takes priority over completing a specific outdoor distance or route.

## 9. Route Suitability

Where practical, the planned route should provide a reasonable combination of:

- safe walking surfaces;
- manageable traffic exposure;
- appropriate crossing conditions;
- shade;
- rest opportunities;
- access to water where needed;
- and the ability to shorten or return safely if conditions change.

- [ ] Route appears appropriate for the dog.
- [ ] Route can reasonably be modified if needed.
- [ ] Route does not depend on unsafe terrain or conditions.
- [ ] The outing can be shortened without compromising safe return.

The 90-minute duration does not require walking continuously for every minute if rest, safety, handling, hydration, or other care needs make pauses appropriate.

## 10. Hydration and Rest

- [ ] Hydration needs have been considered.
- [ ] Rest opportunities are available where appropriate.
- [ ] The dog’s expected pace is realistic.
- [ ] The plan does not require pushing the dog to maintain a predetermined speed, mileage, or performance target.

The goal is safe, appropriate enrichment and exercise, not athletic performance.

## 11. Client Instructions and Normal Routine

- [ ] Client’s normal walking routine has been reviewed.
- [ ] Known restrictions have been reviewed.
- [ ] Known preferred routes or areas to avoid have been reviewed.
- [ ] Known equipment requirements have been reviewed.
- [ ] Client has not requested Cuddle Crew to diagnose, prescribe, or create an exercise program outside normal pet-care scope.

Cuddle Crew may provide an agreed walk based on the pet’s established routine and observed tolerance.

Cuddle Crew does not prescribe exercise beyond the dog’s normal routine or act as a veterinary rehabilitation or canine conditioning provider.

## 12. Day-of-Service Reassessment

Approval for Adventure Walks does not mean every future 90-minute outing must proceed unchanged.

Before or during a scheduled Adventure Walk, modify the plan if the dog shows signs such as:

- unusual fatigue;
- reluctance to continue;
- limping;
- discomfort;
- overheating;
- abnormal breathing;
- distress;
- escalating reactivity;
- equipment problems;
- unsafe weather exposure;
- unsafe surface conditions;
- or another material safety concern.

A previously suitable dog may require a shorter or modified outing on a particular day.

## 13. No Distance or Mileage Guarantee

The 90-Minute Adventure Walk is a time-based Service.

It does not guarantee:

- a specific mileage;
- a specific route;
- a specific pace;
- continuous uninterrupted walking;
- a specific intensity;
- or completion of a predetermined exercise target.

Actual activity should remain appropriate to:

- the dog;
- weather;
- surface conditions;
- behavior;
- safety;
- rest;
- hydration;
- and other circumstances present during the Service.

## 14. Suitability Decision

Select one:

- [ ] **90-MINUTE ADVENTURE WALK APPROPRIATE**
- [ ] **RECOMMEND 60-MINUTE WALK**
- [ ] **RECOMMEND 30-MINUTE WALK**
- [ ] **MODIFIED WALK / ALTERNATIVE CARE**
- [ ] **PERSONALIZED REVIEW REQUIRED**
- [ ] **DECLINE ADVENTURE WALK**

### Conditions / Modifications

Record internally any relevant conditions, such as:

- weather limits;
- route restrictions;
- pace considerations;
- required equipment;
- known triggers;
- rest expectations;
- shorter-duration fallback;
- or other safety considerations.

### Notes

Record only information reasonably necessary for safe Service planning.

Do not record unnecessary medical speculation or diagnose the dog.

## 15. Suitability Principle

Adventure Walk suitability is individualized.

No single factor such as:

- age;
- breed;
- size;
- energy level;
- price;
- or Client preference

automatically determines whether a 90-Minute Adventure Walk is appropriate.

The decision should be based on the complete known care and safety picture.

If the dog cannot reasonably and safely participate in an extended outing, recommend a shorter or modified Service rather than forcing the 90-minute format.

## Website Implementation Notes

The public website may explain the general purpose of the Adventure Walk and that it is subject to individual suitability and day-of safety conditions.

The full internal suitability checklist should not be published.

### Public Service Description

A public description may explain that the 90-Minute Adventure Walk is intended for dogs who are comfortable with longer outings and may be a good fit for:

- dogs accustomed to extended walks;
- dogs who enjoy additional exploration and enrichment;
- dogs whose normal routine supports a longer outing;
- and Clients looking for more extended one-on-one walking time.

Avoid promising that every dog is eligible.

### Appropriate Public Disclaimer

The website may use concise language such as:

**Adventure Walks are subject to individual suitability, weather, behavior, health-related considerations disclosed by the Client, and day-of safety conditions.**

Do not turn this into a medical disclaimer wall or make the Service sound alarming.

### Eligibility UX

The website may collect preliminary information relevant to suitability, such as:

- normal walk duration;
- normal activity level;
- age;
- known mobility restrictions;
- known respiratory or heat sensitivity;
- leash behavior;
- reactivity;
- and Client-provided activity restrictions.

This information is preliminary.

The website must not automatically diagnose the dog or make veterinary judgments.

### Automated Logic

Automation may identify obvious cases that require review.

When review is needed, use:

**Personalized review required**

rather than displaying private internal reasoning.

Do not show a public risk score.

### Duration Recommendations

If the Client selects a 90-Minute Adventure Walk but the available information indicates that a shorter option may be more appropriate, the website may suggest:

- 60-Minute Dog Walk;
- 30-Minute Dog Walk;
- or personalized review.

Do not automatically reject the Client based solely on one answer unless an authoritative rule requires it.

### Weather Behavior

Do not create website language promising that every scheduled Adventure Walk will always include 90 continuous minutes outdoors.

The website should preserve Cuddle Crew’s ability to modify outdoor activity for:

- heat;
- smoke;
- air quality;
- storms;
- unsafe surfaces;
- or other safety conditions.

The Service remains time-based even when the outdoor component must be modified for safety.

### No Mileage Promise

Do not advertise a guaranteed:

- distance;
- mileage;
- route;
- pace;
- or athletic outcome.

If the website includes examples of what an Adventure Walk may look like, label them as examples rather than guarantees.

### Health and Medical Language

Do not make medical claims such as:

- "safe for senior dogs";
- "safe for brachycephalic dogs";
- "good for arthritis";
- "improves heart health";
- or similar health assertions

unless separately reviewed and explicitly approved.

Use individualized, non-diagnostic wording.

### Age

Do not create hard public age cutoffs unless a future CURRENT / APPROVED source explicitly establishes them.

Age is one factor among many.

### Breed

Do not automatically approve or reject based solely on breed.

Brachycephalic and other physical considerations may require more conservative individual review, but the website should not portray breed alone as a diagnosis or automatic outcome.

### Day-of Modification

Client-facing terms should preserve Cuddle Crew’s discretion to:

- shorten outdoor activity;
- choose a safer route;
- take breaks;
- return home;
- substitute safe indoor enrichment;
- or otherwise modify the planned outing

when conditions require it.

### Pricing

The Adventure Walk rate must come from the CURRENT / APPROVED Pricing, Fees & Surcharge Policy.

This suitability document does not create a separate risk fee, health fee, weather fee, or behavior surcharge.

### Precise Petcare

Final Client-specific:

- activity expectations;
- equipment;
- handling notes;
- route restrictions;
- behavior notes;
- and other care instructions

should remain in the appropriate Precise Petcare records.

The website should not become a competing authoritative care-plan database.
