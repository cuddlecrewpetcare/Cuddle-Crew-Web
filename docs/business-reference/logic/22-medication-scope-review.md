## Purpose

This document defines the internal review process used by Cuddle Crew Pet Care LLC to determine whether a requested medication or medication-related care task can be safely and appropriately provided.

The purpose of this review is to distinguish routine medication administration that fits within normal Service from medication needs that require:

- additional time;
- modified care;
- schedule review;
- additional instructions;
- personalized review;
- referral to a more appropriate professional;
- or decline.

This document is an internal operational reference.

The public website may explain that routine medication administration may be available and that advanced, exact-time, high-risk, restraint-intensive, or otherwise complex medication needs require individual review.

The website must not expose the full internal medication-risk rubric or represent Cuddle Crew as providing veterinary diagnosis, treatment, or procedures outside current training, insurance, authorization, and Service scope.

Precise Petcare should remain the operational source of truth for current Client-specific medication instructions and approved care plans.

## Authoritative Content

# MEDICATION ACCEPTANCE / SCOPE REVIEW

Complete this review whenever a medication or medical-care request is not clearly routine and within the normal approved Service scope.

## 1. Identify the Medication or Procedure

Record:

- medication or supplement name;
- purpose as described by the Client or prescribing veterinarian;
- dose;
- route;
- administration method;
- frequency;
- timing;
- acceptable timing window;
- storage requirements;
- and prescribing veterinarian where applicable.

- [ ] Medication/procedure clearly identified.
- [ ] Dose documented.
- [ ] Route documented.
- [ ] Administration method documented.
- [ ] Timing documented.
- [ ] Storage requirements documented where relevant.

Do not guess missing medication instructions.

If essential information is incomplete, the appropriate status is **Pending** until clarified.

## 2. Route and Technique

Identify the requested route or technique.

Examples may include:

- oral pill/capsule;
- medication mixed into food;
- liquid oral medication;
- routine topical medication;
- eye medication;
- ear medication;
- injection;
- injectable medication;
- or another medication-related procedure.

- [ ] Route/technique is understood.
- [ ] Technique is within current competence.
- [ ] Technique is within current Service scope.
- [ ] Technique is compatible with current insurance coverage.
- [ ] Required handling can be completed safely.

Routine oral or topical medication may generally be accepted where:

- instructions are clear;
- the pet can be safely handled;
- the task is within training and scope;
- and the medication fits within the booked Service.

Eye or ear medication requires review of handling requirements and should be accepted only where safe and within current comfort/training.

Injection or injectable medication requires personalized review and should only be accepted where Cuddle Crew is appropriately trained, insured, authorized, and comfortable providing that specific care.

## 3. Client Instructions

- [ ] Client has provided clear written medication instructions.
- [ ] Dose is unambiguous.
- [ ] Timing is unambiguous.
- [ ] Administration method is unambiguous.
- [ ] Storage instructions are clear.
- [ ] Client has explained what counts as successful administration.
- [ ] Client has explained what should happen if the pet refuses.
- [ ] Known reactions or cautions are documented.
- [ ] Prescribing veterinarian contact information is available where appropriate.

Do not improvise medication dose, route, timing, or administration technique.

If instructions conflict or are materially unclear, obtain clarification before acceptance.

## 4. Client Demonstration

Where the medication technique is not obviously routine:

- [ ] Client demonstration completed.
- [ ] Client demonstration not needed.
- [ ] Demonstration still required.

A demonstration may be particularly useful for:

- unusual administration techniques;
- eye/ear medication;
- pets with specific handling routines;
- specialty equipment;
- or medication that the pet accepts only through a particular established method.

A successful Client demonstration does not automatically establish that the Service is within Cuddle Crew’s training, insurance, or safety scope.

## 5. Pet Acceptance

Determine how the pet normally responds.

- [ ] Pet accepts willingly.
- [ ] Pet accepts with mild routine handling.
- [ ] Pet may resist.
- [ ] Pet acceptance is unknown.
- [ ] Pet requires significant restraint.

Consider:

- hiding;
- avoidance;
- growling;
- snapping;
- biting;
- scratching;
- struggling;
- escape behavior;
- guarding;
- and stress escalation.

Medication that is accepted willingly or through normal safe handling may be appropriate.

Medication that requires significant or escalating restraint requires review.

## 6. Restraint Requirement

Select the expected level:

- [ ] **None**
- [ ] **Mild / routine handling**
- [ ] **Significant**
- [ ] **Unknown**

Cuddle Crew will not repeatedly escalate unsafe restraint to force medication administration.

If a pet refuses medication and safe administration cannot be completed:

1. stop escalating restraint;
2. follow the approved medication plan;
3. contact the Client or authorized emergency contact where appropriate;
4. document the unsuccessful attempt and relevant observations;
5. follow any approved backup instructions.

Forceful restraint is not converted into an acceptable Service merely because the Client requests it or is willing to pay more.

## 7. Training and Competence

- [ ] Current training/competence is sufficient.
- [ ] Additional review is required.
- [ ] Current competence is not sufficient.

Consider:

- route;
- technique;
- equipment;
- handling;
- consequences of error;
- Client instructions;
- and the sitter’s actual experience with the procedure.

Do not accept a medication task solely because it is technically possible to attempt.

If competence is insufficient, modify or refer out.

## 8. Insurance and Service Scope

- [ ] Insurance/service scope confirmed.
- [ ] Insurance/service scope requires verification.
- [ ] Requested procedure is outside current approved scope.

Advanced medical procedures, forceful restraint, and high-risk treatment are not part of the normal included Service scope.

A medication task should not be accepted until any material insurance/scope uncertainty has been resolved.

If the requested procedure is not covered or permitted:

- do not perform it;
- do not solve the problem by adding a surcharge;
- and refer the Client to an appropriate professional where useful.

## 9. Timing and Allowed Window

Record:

- prescribed/requested administration time;
- acceptable early window;
- acceptable late window;
- whether the timing is medically or practically flexible;
- and the consequences of delay as provided by the Client or prescribing professional.

- [ ] Timing fits a normal Service window.
- [ ] Timing requires an exact-time commitment.
- [ ] Timing flexibility is known.
- [ ] Timing consequences are understood.

Routine daytime Service windows should not be treated as exact-minute guarantees.

If medication requires exact-time administration, the request requires personalized review unless specifically approved.

## 10. Exact-Time Medication

If exact-time medication is requested:

- [ ] Exact time is genuinely required.
- [ ] Acceptable timing tolerance is documented.
- [ ] Route and travel reliability are workable.
- [ ] Other confirmed Client obligations remain workable.
- [ ] Required administration can be performed reliably.
- [ ] Consequences of delay are understood.

Exact-time medication is a review trigger.

Do not automatically promise exact-minute administration merely because the Client enters a time into a website form.

Possible outcomes include:

- specifically approved exact-time care;
- modified timing;
- alternate care plan;
- referral;
- or decline.

## 11. High-Risk Consequences

Determine whether a missed, delayed, incomplete, or unsuccessful dose could carry materially elevated consequences.

- [ ] No known elevated consequence.
- [ ] Elevated consequence disclosed.
- [ ] Consequence is unclear and requires clarification.

High-risk medication requires personalized review.

The review should consider:

- schedule reliability;
- administration reliability;
- pet cooperation;
- sitter competence;
- insurance/scope;
- emergency backup;
- and consequences of a missed or delayed dose.

Do not independently diagnose the medical importance of a medication.

Use information supplied by the Client and prescribing veterinary professional where applicable.

## 12. Complex or Controlled Regimen

Personalized review is required where the care involves a materially complex medical regimen, including situations such as:

- multiple medications with different timing requirements;
- complicated preparation;
- advanced technique;
- high-risk consequences;
- unusual monitoring requirements;
- or care that materially exceeds routine pet-sitting medication administration.

Such cases may require referral to:

- a veterinary technician pet sitter;
- a medical-needs pet sitter;
- a veterinary provider;
- or another appropriate professional category.

Referral does not imply endorsement of a specific provider unless Cuddle Crew has a verified referral relationship.

## 13. Medication Refusal Plan

- [ ] Client has provided instructions if the pet refuses medication.
- [ ] Backup method is documented where appropriate.
- [ ] Emergency contact pathway is known.
- [ ] Veterinarian escalation instructions are known where appropriate.

If a pet refuses medication during a booking:

- do not repeatedly escalate unsafe restraint;
- use only approved alternative methods;
- contact the Client or emergency contact as appropriate;
- document what occurred;
- and follow the agreed care plan.

Do not invent an alternate dosing technique.

## 14. Service Duration

Determine whether the medication can reasonably fit within the requested Service duration.

- [ ] Medication fits comfortably within the booked duration.
- [ ] Medication may require additional time.
- [ ] Longer Service is required.
- [ ] Duration is uncertain and requires review.

Routine agreed medication is included where it fits the booked Service.

If medication consistently requires materially more time, use a longer Service rather than inventing a medication surcharge.

Current daytime duration ladder:

- 30 minutes
- 60 minutes
- 90 minutes
- personalized/custom care where standard durations do not fit

Do not materially shorten other promised pet care merely to fit medication into an inadequate duration.

## 15. Interaction With Other Care Tasks

Consider the complete Service routine, including:

- feeding;
- water;
- walking;
- potty care;
- litter/enclosure care;
- enrichment;
- cleanup;
- separation;
- documentation;
- and medication.

- [ ] Complete routine still fits the Service duration.
- [ ] Medication causes another required task to no longer fit.
- [ ] Service plan must be modified.

Medication acceptance cannot be evaluated in isolation if it materially affects the rest of the booked care.

## 16. Behavior Interaction

If medication handling creates or increases behavior risk:

- [ ] Behavior remains safely manageable.
- [ ] Behavior Risk Review required.
- [ ] Safe administration is not workable.

Examples include:

- guarding medication or food;
- handling sensitivity;
- growling;
- snapping;
- biting;
- scratching;
- escape attempts;
- escalating restraint;
- or defensive behavior.

If behavior risk materially changes the medication plan, use the Behavior Risk Review in addition to this document.

## 17. Emergency Planning

Where the medication is medically important or high-risk:

- [ ] Emergency contact hierarchy is current.
- [ ] Veterinary information is current.
- [ ] Emergency Veterinary Authorization is complete where applicable.
- [ ] Client instructions for missed/refused medication are documented.
- [ ] Applicable emergency escalation pathway is understood.

Medication instructions do not replace the signed Emergency Veterinary Authorization.

Cuddle Crew does not diagnose or independently change veterinary treatment plans.

## 18. Medication Pricing Principle

Do **not** create a generic medication, medical-risk, or medical-complexity surcharge solely because medication is involved.

Use this principle:

**More time → use more booked time.**

**More risk, medical complexity, or scope uncertainty → review whether Cuddle Crew should provide the care at all.**

Routine medication that fits the booked Service remains part of that Service where approved.

Additional pricing should arise from legitimate additional Service time or another existing CURRENT / APPROVED pricing rule, not an arbitrary medical fee.

## 19. Common Internal Responses

### Pill / Capsule Given Willingly or in Food

Generally acceptable where:

- instructions are clear;
- dose is clear;
- pet can be safely handled;
- and administration fits the Service.

### Supplement Mixed Into Food

Generally acceptable where:

- Client provides clear preparation and dosing instructions;
- and administration fits the Service.

### Routine Topical Medication

Generally acceptable where:

- within training;
- pet tolerates application;
- and administration is safe.

### Eye / Ear Medication

Requires handling review.

Accept only where:

- safe;
- within training;
- pet handling is manageable;
- and the technique is appropriate for Cuddle Crew’s Service scope.

### Injection / Injectable Medication

Personalized review.

Accept only where:

- current training is sufficient;
- insurance/service scope permits;
- proper authorization exists;
- technique is understood;
- and Cuddle Crew is comfortable providing the specific care.

### Forceful Restraint Required

Do not automatically accept.

Reassess the plan and decline unsafe administration where necessary.

### Exact-Time / High-Risk Medication

Personalized review.

Schedule reliability and consequences of delay matter.

### Controlled or Complex Medical Regimen

Personalized review.

May require referral to a veterinary or medical-needs professional.

### Medication Refused During Booking

Do not repeatedly escalate unsafe restraint.

Follow the approved refusal/backup plan and contact the Client or emergency contact as appropriate.

Record internally as applicable:

- longer Service;
- different Service time;
- defined timing window;
- Client demonstration;
- additional written instructions;
- approved handling technique;
- backup medication plan;
- emergency-contact update;
- veterinary clarification;

### Rationale

Document concise operational rationale related to:

- safety;
- timing;
- training;
- insurance/scope;
- pet cooperation;
- Service duration;
- or care-plan feasibility.

Do not write unnecessary medical speculation or diagnoses.

## 21. Medication Acceptance Principle

Medication should be accepted only when Cuddle Crew can reasonably provide it:

- safely;
- reliably;
- within current training and competence;
- within current insurance and Service scope;
- according to clear Client/veterinary instructions;
- within a workable timing window;
- without escalating unsafe restraint;
- and within a Service plan that still allows the pet’s other required care to be completed.

If the medication requires materially more time, use more Service time.

If the medication creates materially greater risk, advanced medical scope, unreliable exact-time requirements, or unsafe handling, use personalized review, modification, referral, or decline rather than adding an arbitrary surcharge.

## Website Implementation Notes

This document should control internal medication-review behavior but should not be published as a full public medication-risk rubric.

### Public Medication Description

The website may state generally that:

- routine agreed oral or topical medication may be available;
- medication must be discussed in advance;
- some medications require additional review;
- and advanced, high-risk, exact-time, restraint-intensive, injectable, or complex care may be unavailable or require a different provider.

Keep the public wording concise and non-diagnostic.

### Website Intake Fields

Website or onboarding forms may collect preliminary information such as:

- pet;
- medication/supplement;
- dose;
- route;
- frequency;
- requested timing;
- acceptable timing window;
- storage;
- administration method;
- whether the pet normally accepts it;
- whether restraint is normally required;
- prescribing veterinarian;
- and special instructions.

Do not treat form submission as medication approval.

### Automatic Review Triggers

Website logic may route medication to personalized review when it detects conditions such as:

- injection/injectable medication;
- eye/ear medication requiring difficult handling;
- exact-time medication;
- high-risk medication;
- significant restraint;
- complex regimen;
- advanced procedure;
- unclear route;
- unclear instructions;
- or another non-routine medical-care request.

The public result should ordinarily be:

**Personalized review required**

rather than exposing the internal risk reason in detail.

### Do Not Diagnose

The website must not:

- diagnose conditions;
- recommend medication;
- recommend dose changes;
- interpret veterinary instructions independently;
- tell Clients to skip medication;
- or substitute veterinary guidance.

### Timing UX

Do not allow a Client to create an automatic exact-time guarantee merely by choosing a precise clock time.

Where an exact-time need is indicated:

1. collect the requested time;
2. collect the acceptable timing window if known;
3. mark the medication for personalized review;
4. do not confirm exact-time performance until specifically approved.

### Restraint

Do not advertise or imply that Cuddle Crew will force medication through significant restraint.

Client-facing wording may explain that medication must be safely administrable.

If significant restraint is indicated, route to review.

### Pricing

Medication logic must not create a generic medication fee.

If more time is required, the website may recommend:

- 60 minutes instead of 30;
- 90 minutes instead of 60;
- or personalized/custom care.

Do not add an automatic:

- medical fee;
- medication fee;
- high-risk fee;
- injection fee;
- restraint fee;
- or complexity surcharge

unless a future CURRENT / APPROVED pricing policy expressly creates one.

### Referral

When needs are outside Cuddle Crew’s scope, the website may use neutral language such as:

**This care may be better suited to a veterinary or medical-needs pet-care professional.**

Do not claim endorsement of a specific provider unless a verified referral relationship exists.

### Precise Petcare Handoff

Final medication instructions and approved scope should remain in Precise Petcare.

The website should not become a competing authoritative medication-record system.

Any preliminary website medication information should be reviewed and reconciled into the approved Precise Petcare record before Service.

### Privacy

Do not expose:

- medication details;
- medical conditions;
- veterinary information;
- dosing schedules;
- medical-risk notes;
- or internal medication-review results

in public pages, URLs, analytics, frontend logs, or browser-visible configuration.

### Conflict Handling

If website behavior conflicts with this CURRENT / APPROVED Medication Scope Review:

1. flag the conflict;
2. identify the affected form, estimator, or booking logic;
3. preserve the safety/scope rule;
4. do not weaken medication review merely to keep automatic booking available.

If this document conflicts with a CURRENT / APPROVED Client-facing agreement, Medication Administration Consent, Emergency Veterinary Authorization, or other policy, the Client-facing document controls the contractual subject and the conflict should be flagged for human review.
