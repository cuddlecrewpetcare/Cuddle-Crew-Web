## Purpose

This document defines the internal behavior and handling review used by Cuddle Crew Pet Care LLC when a pet, household, or requested Service presents a material behavior or safety consideration.

The purpose of this review is to determine whether care can be provided safely and reliably and whether the Service should be:

- accepted as requested;
- accepted with conditions;
- modified;
- referred to another professional;
- declined;
- or kept pending while additional information is gathered.

This document is an internal operational reference.

It is not intended to create a public risk score, behavior label, temperament grade, or diagnostic assessment.

The public website may collect relevant Client disclosures and route certain cases to personalized review, but it should not expose the full internal behavior rubric, internal severity ratings, sitter-safety analysis, mitigation reasoning, or decline criteria.

Cuddle Crew does not diagnose behavior disorders and does not replace a qualified trainer, behavior consultant, veterinary behavior professional, or veterinarian.

Precise Petcare should remain the operational source of truth for current Client-specific behavior disclosures, handling instructions, equipment requirements, and approved care plans.

## Authoritative Content

# BEHAVIOR RISK REVIEW

Complete this review when a pet, household, or requested Service presents a material behavior, handling, escape, separation, or sitter-safety concern.

## 1. Bite / Snap History

Review:

- [ ] No known bite or snap history.
- [ ] Prior snap or attempted bite disclosed.
- [ ] Prior bite disclosed.
- [ ] Serious bite history disclosed.
- [ ] Details are incomplete and require clarification.

Document where relevant:

- context;
- trigger;
- target;
- severity as described by the Client;
- recency;
- frequency;
- warning signals;
- and known management strategy.

A known serious bite history requires pre-booking safety review.

A bite or snap history is not automatically an acceptance or decline decision by itself, but materially elevated risk may require:

- modified care;
- additional conditions;
- referral;
- or decline.

Do not minimize a known bite history merely because the pet appears calm during one interaction.

## 2. Resource Guarding

Review whether the pet guards:

- food;
- treats;
- toys;
- beds;
- crates;
- people;
- spaces;
- doors;
- or other resources.

- [ ] No known guarding concern.
- [ ] Mild/manageable guarding disclosed.
- [ ] Material guarding concern disclosed.
- [ ] Guarding is unpredictable or unclear.

If guarding behavior escalates:

- do not forcibly remove the guarded item;
- create distance where appropriate;
- use only approved safe management;
- and stop the triggering task if necessary.

A care plan requiring repeated confrontation over guarded resources is not acceptable merely because the task is requested.

## 3. Leash Reactivity and Control

Where walking is requested, review:

- pulling strength;
- leash reactivity;
- lunging;
- barking;
- freezing;
- bolting;
- fear;
- dog-directed reactions;
- person-directed reactions;
- wildlife reactions;
- vehicle/bicycle reactions;
- and ability to redirect safely.

- [ ] Dog remains safely controllable.
- [ ] Management modification may be required.
- [ ] Significant leash-control concern exists.
- [ ] Dog cannot currently be safely leashed or walked.

A dog that cannot safely be leashed must not be forced into a walk.

Where walking is unsafe, possible alternatives include:

- safe potty care;
- yard time where appropriate;
- indoor enrichment;
- modified Service;
- behavior review;
- referral;
- or decline.

Do not promise a walk merely because the Client booked or paid for one.

## 4. Escape / Door-Dashing Risk

Review:

- door-dashing;
- gate rushing;
- slipping harnesses/collars;
- fence climbing;
- digging;
- bolting;
- crate escape;
- window escape;
- enclosure escape;
- and history of becoming loose.

- [ ] No material known escape concern.
- [ ] Manageable escape precautions required.
- [ ] Elevated escape risk.
- [ ] Existing containment/access plan is not reliable.

Consider whether:

- doors can be safely managed;
- gates latch securely;
- walking equipment is secure;
- crates/enclosures are appropriate;
- separation zones are workable;
- and backup containment exists where appropriate.

If escape risk materially increases, require a safer containment/access plan before future routine Service.

Do not improvise unsafe containment.

## 5. Handling Sensitivity

Review known sensitivity to:

- touching;
- paws;
- face;
- ears;
- collar;
- harness;
- leash attachment;
- picking up;
- grooming-like handling;
- medication;
- crate handling;
- or other routine care.

- [ ] Routine handling is generally tolerated.
- [ ] Mild sensitivity requires accommodation.
- [ ] Significant sensitivity exists.
- [ ] Required task cannot be completed safely.

Use low-pressure handling and approved techniques.

Do not escalate force merely to complete a non-emergency task.

If the required care depends on handling that cannot be completed safely, modify, refer, or decline.

## 6. Fear of Strangers / Hiding

Review:

- hiding;
- avoidance;
- freezing;
- defensive behavior;
- fear barking;
- inability to approach;
- and response to unfamiliar people.

Mild fear or hiding does not automatically make care unsuitable.

Where safe:

- use a low-pressure approach;
- allow reasonable space;
- avoid forcing interaction;
- complete essential care;
- and document observations.

If fear escalates into unsafe handling or prevents essential care, review the future plan.

## 7. Pet-to-Pet Conflict and Separation

Review:

- feeding conflicts;
- fights;
- tension;
- resource competition;
- redirected aggression;
- play that becomes unsafe;
- required room separation;
- crate rotation;
- and other household management needs.

- [ ] Pets can safely share normal care areas.
- [ ] Simple separation plan required.
- [ ] Complex separation/rotation required.
- [ ] Separation plan is unreliable or unsafe.

Required separation must be explicit and operationally workable.

Do not improvise unsafe rotations.

If a complex separation routine materially changes the Service duration or safety profile, require:

- longer Service;
- modified care plan;
- personalized review;
- or decline.

## 8. Predator / Prey Risk

Where different species or vulnerable animals share a household, review:

- physical separation;
- enclosure security;
- doors/gates;
- rotation order;
- feeding order;
- and what must never be opened or combined.

- [ ] No material predator/prey concern.
- [ ] Clear and reliable separation plan exists.
- [ ] Separation requires heightened care.
- [ ] Separation plan is unreliable or unsafe.

Predator/prey separation that is complex or unreliable requires personalized review.

Do not improvise a new rotation or allow contact that the Client has not specifically approved.

## 9. Unsafe Household / Person Risk

Review whether any household condition or person creates a material safety concern, including:

- threatening behavior;
- harassment;
- intoxication;
- undisclosed occupant;
- unsafe visitor;
- unsafe property condition;
- unsecured weapon;
- dangerous access condition;
- or another situation that reasonably threatens sitter safety.

- [ ] No known material person/property concern.
- [ ] Concern can be resolved before Service.
- [ ] Material safety concern requires review.
- [ ] Immediate entry/continued presence would be unsafe.

Sitter safety takes priority.

If an unexpected person or unsafe condition is present:

- decline entry or leave when reasonably necessary;
- contact the Client or emergency contact as appropriate;
- document the situation;
- and review future Service.

Payment does not require Cuddle Crew to remain in an unsafe environment.

## 10. Medication-Related Handling Behavior

If medication is involved, review whether the pet:

- accepts medication willingly;
- tolerates routine handling;
- hides;
- guards;
- struggles;
- scratches;
- growls;
- snaps;
- bites;
- or requires significant restraint.

- [ ] Medication handling is safely manageable.
- [ ] Medication Scope Review required.
- [ ] Medication cannot be safely administered under the current plan.

Medication will not be forced through escalating unsafe restraint.

If the pet refuses and safe administration is not possible:

- stop escalating;
- follow the approved medication-refusal plan;
- contact the Client/emergency contact where appropriate;
- and document the outcome.

## 11. Equipment and Mitigation

Identify any approved mitigation or equipment required for safe care, such as:

- secure harness;
- secure collar;
- backup attachment;
- gate;
- baby gate;
- crate;
- room separation;
- leash setup;
- muzzle already appropriately conditioned and Client-directed where within scope;
- visual barriers;
- low-stimulation route;
- or another established safety measure.

- [ ] Required equipment is available.
- [ ] Equipment is functional.
- [ ] Equipment was reviewed or tested where appropriate.
- [ ] Mitigation does not depend on forceful handling.
- [ ] Client instructions are clear.

Do not invent or introduce specialized behavior equipment without appropriate Client approval and scope review.

Do not rely on equipment alone where the underlying behavior remains unsafe.

## 12. Service-Type Fit

Determine whether the requested Service remains appropriate.

Examples:

### Drop-In Visit

May remain appropriate where essential care can be completed safely with appropriate handling and management.

### Dog Walk

Requires safe leash attachment, control, entry/exit, and outdoor handling.

If these are not workable, modify the plan rather than forcing the walk.

### Adventure Walk

Requires additional suitability because an extended outing increases duration and environmental exposure.

Use the Adventure Walk Suitability Review where applicable.

### Overnight Care

Requires behavior to remain workable for prolonged evening-through-morning presence, movement throughout the home, nighttime routines, and safe entry/exit.

Use the Overnight Acceptance Review where applicable.

## 13. Duration Fit

- [ ] Behavior-management needs fit within the booked duration.
- [ ] Additional setup/separation time is required.
- [ ] Longer Service is required.
- [ ] Standard duration may not fit.

If safe management materially increases Service time, use a longer appropriate Service where the issue is time.

Do not simply rush other required pet care.

If the issue is elevated behavior risk rather than time, use safety review, modification, referral, or decline rather than inventing a behavior surcharge.

## 14. Client-Provided Management Plan

- [ ] Client has provided clear handling instructions.
- [ ] Known triggers are documented.
- [ ] Known calming/de-escalation strategies are documented.
- [ ] Required separations are documented.
- [ ] Prohibited interactions are documented.
- [ ] Approved alternatives are documented where normal care cannot be completed.

Do not rely on vague instructions such as:

- "he'll probably be fine";
- "just make him";
- "she only bites sometimes";
- or similar language

where material safety questions remain unresolved.

Obtain specific information before acceptance.

## 15. Day-of Behavior Changes

A previously accepted pet may behave differently on a future Service.

If behavior escalates during care:

- stop the triggering task;
- create distance where appropriate;
- protect personal and animal safety;
- use approved safe alternatives where available;
- contact the Client if needed;
- document the change;
- and review future Services.

A prior successful visit does not guarantee that future behavior will remain unchanged.

## 16. Incident Escalation

If behavior results in:

- bite attempt;
- snap;
- bite;
- scratch;
- escape;
- near escape;
- serious fight;
- unsafe human interaction;
- or another material safety event,

follow the appropriate incident documentation process.

Future Service should be reviewed before routine care continues where the incident materially changes known risk.

## 17. Internal Risk Assessment

For internal review only, each applicable factor may be considered:

- **Low**
- **Moderate**
- **High**

Factors include:

- bite/snap history;
- resource guarding;
- leash reactivity/control;
- escape risk;
- handling sensitivity;
- pet-to-pet separation;
- predator/prey risk;
- unsafe household/person risk.

These ratings are internal decision aids only.

They must not be treated as a scientific score, diagnostic label, legal classification, or public Client/pet rating.

A single "High" factor does not automatically create one predetermined outcome.

Consider:

- severity;
- context;
- predictability;
- management reliability;
- required Service;
- equipment;
- Client disclosure;
- prior incidents;
- and whether safe mitigation is realistically available.

## 18. Mitigation Review

Record internally:

- required equipment;
- handling modification;
- separation plan;
- route modification;
- duration modification;
- alternate task;
- Client action required;
- or other mitigation.

Ask:

- Does the mitigation actually reduce the material risk?
- Is it realistic every visit?
- Does it depend on perfect Client preparation?
- Can it be performed without force?
- Does it remain within training and insurance scope?
- Does it fit the booked duration?

If the answer is uncertain, do not treat the mitigation as sufficient merely because it exists on paper.

## 19. Referral

Referral may be appropriate where needs exceed normal pet-sitting scope.

Possible referral categories include:

- qualified trainer;
- behavior consultant;
- veterinary behavior professional;
- veterinary provider;
- medical-needs pet sitter;
- species-specific professional;
- or another appropriate provider category.

Referral does not mean endorsing a specific provider unless Cuddle Crew has personally verified the referral relationship.

## 20. Behavior Pricing Principle

Do **not** create a generic behavior-risk surcharge.

Use this principle:

**More time → use more booked time where appropriate.**

**More behavior risk → review safety, handling, equipment, Service fit, referral, or decline.**

A higher fee does not make unsafe handling acceptable.

Do not solve aggression, escape risk, unsafe restraint, or sitter-safety concerns by simply adding a surcharge.

Select one:

- [ ] **ACCEPT**
- [ ] **ACCEPT WITH CONDITIONS**
- [ ] **MODIFY SERVICE PLAN**
- [ ] **LONGER SERVICE REQUIRED**
- [ ] **PERSONALIZED REVIEW REQUIRED**
- [ ] **REFER**
- [ ] **DECLINE**
- [ ] **PENDING INFORMATION**

### Mitigation / Equipment

Record internally:

- approved equipment;
- separation requirements;
- handling limitations;
- route limitations;
- alternate care;
- access requirements;
- or other safety conditions.

### Future Booking Conditions

Record any future conditions such as:

- Meet & Greet/reorientation required;
- updated behavior disclosure;
- equipment change;
- no walking;
- shorter walk;
- specific separation plan;
- additional Service time;
- Client presence for reassessment;
- or future review after an incident.

### Notes

Document concise facts relevant to safety and care.

Avoid unnecessary subjective or stigmatizing language.

## 22. Behavior Review Principle

Behavior risk should be assessed according to the actual care task and circumstances.

The goal is not to label a pet as "good," "bad," "aggressive," or "safe."

The goal is to answer:

- Can this specific care be provided safely?
- Can required handling be completed reliably?
- Can known triggers be managed?
- Is containment reliable?
- Can the requested Service be completed without forceful or unsafe handling?
- Does the plan remain within training, insurance, and Service scope?

If safe handling cannot reasonably be assured, modify, refer, or decline.

## Website Implementation Notes

The website may collect relevant behavior information and use it to trigger human review, but should not reproduce the internal Behavior Risk Review publicly.

### Public Behavior Questions

Website or onboarding forms may ask Clients about:

- bite or snap history;
- resource guarding;
- leash reactivity or pulling;
- escape/door-dashing;
- stranger fear;
- handling sensitivity;
- pet-to-pet conflict;
- separation needs;
- predator/prey concerns;
- and equipment requirements.

Use neutral, factual wording.

Avoid language that sounds accusatory or judgmental.

### No Public Scoring

Do not display:

- Low / Moderate / High;
- risk score;
- danger score;
- aggression score;
- acceptance probability;
- sitter-safety score;
- or similar internal classification.

The Client-facing result should instead use states such as:

- Information received
- Additional information needed
- Under review
- Personalized review required

### Review Triggers

A website response indicating material:

- bite history;
- serious guarding;
- unsafe leash control;
- escape risk;
- difficult handling;
- complex separation;
- predator/prey management;
- or unsafe household circumstances

may route the request to internal review.

Do not automatically decline from one form answer unless a separate CURRENT / APPROVED rule specifically requires that result.

### Walking Logic

If submitted information indicates that a dog may not be safely leashable or controllable:

- do not automatically confirm a Dog Walk;
- route to personalized review;
- and allow consideration of alternative care.

Do not publicly say "your dog failed the walking assessment."

### Medication Interaction

If Client responses indicate medication requires significant restraint or creates bite/scratch risk, route to both:

- Medication Scope Review;
- and Behavior Risk Review where applicable.

Do not auto-approve because the medication itself is otherwise routine.

### Separation Logic

If a Client indicates complex pet-to-pet or predator/prey separation:

- route to personalized review;
- do not assume an additional-pet fee resolves the issue;
- do not expose the internal complexity threshold.

### Service-Duration Logic

Where behavior-management or separation needs materially increase required time, the website may recommend:

- longer Service;
- modified Service;
- or personalized review.

Do not add a behavior surcharge unless a future CURRENT / APPROVED pricing policy explicitly creates one.

### Sitter Safety

Do not ask Clients to evaluate or approve Cuddle Crew’s personal safety threshold.

The website may ask for factual disclosures, but final sitter-safety decisions remain internal.

### Language

Prefer public wording such as:

**Some behavior or handling needs require a personalized review so we can make sure the care plan is safe and appropriate.**

Avoid public labels such as:

- dangerous;
- aggressive;
- high risk;
- problem dog;
- bad behavior;
- unsafe pet.

### Day-of Changes

Client-facing policies should preserve Cuddle Crew’s ability to modify or discontinue an unsafe task if behavior changes during Service.

The website must not imply that a confirmed booking obligates Cuddle Crew to continue unsafe handling.

### Precise Petcare Handoff

Final behavior information should remain in Precise Petcare, including:

- known triggers;
- handling notes;
- equipment;
- separation;
- escape precautions;
- walking restrictions;
- and approved alternatives.

The website should not become a competing authoritative behavior-record system.

### Privacy

Do not expose:

- bite history;
- behavior notes;
- internal risk assessment;
- incident history;
- household-safety notes;
- mitigation strategy;
- or decline rationale

in public pages, analytics, URLs, browser logs, or frontend configuration.

### Conflict Handling

If website logic conflicts with this CURRENT / APPROVED Behavior Risk Review:

1. flag the conflict;
2. identify the affected form or booking path;
3. preserve the safety rule;
4. do not weaken behavior review merely to allow automated booking.

If this document conflicts with a CURRENT / APPROVED Client-facing agreement, behavior disclosure, or safety policy, the Client-facing document controls the contractual subject and the conflict should be flagged for human review.
