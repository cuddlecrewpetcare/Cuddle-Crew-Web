## Purpose

This document defines the internal coordination process used by Cuddle Crew Pet Care LLC when another person or provider will also participate in pet care, home access, medication, emergency response, or other responsibilities during a Cuddle Crew booking.

Shared care may involve:

- another professional pet sitter;
- dog walker;
- family member;
- friend;
- neighbor;
- house sitter;
- veterinary professional;
- home service provider;
- or another responsible person who will have access to the property or responsibility for part of the care plan.

The purpose of this review is to ensure that:

- every caregiver is identified;
- responsibilities are clearly divided;
- medication responsibility is explicit;
- access arrangements do not conflict;
- communication is workable;
- emergency responsibility is understood;
- the complete care schedule remains adequate;
- and there is a defined plan if another caregiver does not perform their assigned care.

This document is an internal operational reference.

The public website may ask whether another caregiver will be involved and may explain that shared-care arrangements require clear coordination, but it should not expose private third-party information, internal responsibility notes, access details, or internal decision reasoning.

Precise Petcare should remain the operational source of truth for current Client-specific care instructions, schedules, access information, shared-care details, and confirmed booking records.

## Authoritative Content

# SHARED-CARE COORDINATION CHECKLIST

Complete this review whenever another person or provider will share responsibility for a Client’s pets, home, or care schedule during a Cuddle Crew booking.

## 1. Identify Every Other Caregiver

For each other caregiver or provider, record internally:

- name;
- relationship to Client or company;
- contact information;
- role;
- dates or times involved;
- and general responsibilities.

- [ ] Every known other caregiver has been identified.
- [ ] Contact information is available where reasonably necessary.
- [ ] Role or relationship is understood.
- [ ] Dates/times of involvement are reasonably clear.

Do not rely on vague descriptions such as:

- “my neighbor might stop by”;
- “a friend may check on them”;
- “someone in the family will probably handle it”;
- or similar uncertain arrangements.

A person who only **might** provide care is not treated as a reliable part of the care plan.

## 2. Divide Responsibilities Explicitly

Every recurring responsibility should be assigned to Cuddle Crew, the other caregiver, or clearly shared where appropriate.

Review:

- [ ] Morning feeding / potty.
- [ ] Midday care.
- [ ] Evening feeding / potty.
- [ ] Dog walks.
- [ ] Medication.
- [ ] Fresh water.
- [ ] Litter or enclosure care.
- [ ] Exercise / enrichment.
- [ ] Separation or rotation.
- [ ] Home/security tasks.
- [ ] Emergency response.
- [ ] Other relevant care.

Avoid overlapping assumptions.

For each material task, it should be reasonably clear:

- who is responsible;
- when they are responsible;
- what they are expected to do;
- and what happens if they cannot perform it.

## 3. Expected Times and Care Windows

- [ ] Expected timing for Cuddle Crew Services is understood.
- [ ] Expected timing for the other caregiver is understood.
- [ ] Medication timing responsibilities are explicit.
- [ ] Feeding timing responsibilities are explicit where relevant.
- [ ] Potty/elimination timing remains adequate.
- [ ] The combined schedule does not create unreasonable care gaps.

Do not treat uncertain statements such as “they’ll probably come sometime in the afternoon” as reliable where animal welfare depends on that visit.

Where exact timing matters, responsibility and timing must be sufficiently clear.

## 4. Medication Responsibility

For every medication:

- [ ] Responsible caregiver has been identified.
- [ ] Dose responsibility is clear.
- [ ] Timing responsibility is clear.
- [ ] Administration method is clear.
- [ ] There is no reasonable risk of duplicate administration.
- [ ] There is no reasonable risk that everyone assumes someone else administered it.
- [ ] Missed-dose communication plan is understood where relevant.

Medication responsibility must not remain ambiguous.

If both Cuddle Crew and another caregiver may administer medication, the handoff/documentation process must be clear enough to prevent:

- duplicate dosing;
- missed dosing;
- incorrect timing;
- or contradictory instructions.

Complex medication-sharing arrangements may require personalized review.

## 5. Feeding Responsibility

Where feeding is shared:

- [ ] Feeding schedule is clearly divided.
- [ ] Portion responsibilities are clear.
- [ ] Special-diet responsibilities are clear.
- [ ] Food preparation responsibilities are clear.
- [ ] Pet separation during meals is clear where relevant.
- [ ] There is a way to identify whether feeding has already occurred where necessary.

Avoid arrangements where multiple caregivers may unknowingly feed the same meal or assume another person already fed the pet.

## 6. Water Responsibility

- [ ] Water-refresh responsibility is understood.
- [ ] Automatic fountain/equipment responsibility is understood where relevant.
- [ ] Equipment failure plan is understood where needed.
- [ ] Cuddle Crew remains able to provide appropriate water care during booked Services regardless of third-party assumptions.

Automatic equipment may support the care plan but does not remove the need for appropriate welfare checks.

## 7. Potty / Elimination Responsibility

For dogs:

- [ ] Potty opportunities remain adequate across the combined schedule.
- [ ] Responsibility for each expected care period is clear.

For cats and other pets:

- [ ] Litter/enclosure/elimination responsibilities are divided.
- [ ] Frequency remains appropriate.

Do not reduce the overall care plan below what Cuddle Crew considers adequate merely because another person may be involved.

The full schedule—not only Cuddle Crew’s portion—must remain appropriate for animal welfare.

## 8. Walk Responsibilities

Where another person will also walk a dog:

- [ ] Walking responsibilities are clearly divided.
- [ ] Equipment arrangements are understood.
- [ ] Any walking restrictions are shared where appropriate.
- [ ] Behavior or reactivity concerns are known to the Client and appropriately managed.
- [ ] The combined activity plan does not create obvious unnecessary duplication or conflict.

Cuddle Crew is responsible only for the Services assigned to Cuddle Crew.

## 9. Access Coordination

- [ ] Cuddle Crew’s access method is confirmed.
- [ ] Other caregiver’s access method is understood where relevant.
- [ ] Access methods do not conflict.
- [ ] Keys will not be removed or relocated unexpectedly.
- [ ] Lockbox expectations are clear.
- [ ] Codes will not be changed without appropriate notice.
- [ ] Alarm responsibility is understood.
- [ ] Gate/garage/concierge requirements are workable.

Shared care must not create a situation where one caregiver:

- takes the only key;
- changes a code;
- locks a door Cuddle Crew cannot reopen;
- arms an alarm unexpectedly;
- removes access equipment;
- or otherwise prevents scheduled care.

Client-specific access information must remain securely stored in the approved operational system.

## 10. Home Security Responsibilities

Where home tasks are divided:

- [ ] Door-lock responsibilities are clear.
- [ ] Alarm responsibilities are clear.
- [ ] Gate/garage responsibilities are clear.
- [ ] Light/blind/security routines are understood where applicable.
- [ ] No third party is expected to alter security arrangements without appropriate communication.

Do not assume Cuddle Crew is responsible for correcting third-party home-security changes outside the agreed Service scope.

## 11. Communication Plan

- [ ] Primary Client communication method is established.
- [ ] Need for direct caregiver-to-caregiver communication has been determined.
- [ ] Appropriate contact information is available where needed.
- [ ] Material care changes have a defined reporting pathway.
- [ ] Medication or emergency updates have a clear communication pathway.
- [ ] Privacy limits are understood.

The Client should remain the primary coordinator unless another arrangement is specifically approved.

Cuddle Crew is not required to manage another caregiver’s entire schedule or act as a general dispatcher for third parties unless that responsibility is specifically accepted.

## 12. Care Documentation

Where coordination depends on knowing what another caregiver completed:

- [ ] Documentation method is established where necessary.
- [ ] Medication completion can be verified where required.
- [ ] Feeding completion can be verified where required.
- [ ] Material care changes can be communicated.
- [ ] Cuddle Crew’s own journals remain accurate to the care Cuddle Crew actually provided.

Do not document another caregiver’s work as though Cuddle Crew personally performed it.

Cuddle Crew journals should distinguish between:

- directly observed information;
- care personally provided;
- and information reported by the Client or another caregiver.

## 13. Emergency Authority and Contact Hierarchy

- [ ] Client emergency contact hierarchy is understood.
- [ ] Veterinary authority remains governed by the applicable signed Emergency Veterinary Authorization.
- [ ] Role of the other caregiver during an emergency is understood.
- [ ] Cuddle Crew’s emergency responsibilities are understood.
- [ ] No conflicting emergency authority is apparent.
- [ ] Communication between caregivers is workable if an emergency occurs.

A third party’s presence does not expand Cuddle Crew’s legal or financial veterinary authority.

The signed Emergency Veterinary Authorization controls Cuddle Crew’s applicable authority.

## 14. Emergency Decision Conflicts

If another caregiver gives instructions that conflict with:

- the Client’s current approved instructions;
- signed emergency authorization;
- the confirmed booking;
- or another authoritative Client record,

do not silently choose between conflicting instructions.

Where practical:

1. prioritize immediate animal and human safety;
2. contact the Client;
3. use the authorized emergency-contact hierarchy where necessary;
4. follow signed emergency authority;
5. document the conflict and resolution.

## 15. Care Frequency as a Whole

Review the complete shared-care schedule.

- [ ] Combined care frequency is adequate.
- [ ] Feeding frequency is adequate.
- [ ] Potty/elimination opportunities are adequate.
- [ ] Medication timing is adequate.
- [ ] Water monitoring is adequate.
- [ ] Relevant companionship/welfare checks are adequate.
- [ ] No material care gap depends on an uncertain third-party visit.

A shared-care arrangement should be assessed as one complete care plan.

Do not accept inadequate Cuddle Crew frequency merely because an unidentified or unreliable person may supplement care.

## 16. Third-Party No-Show Plan

A no-show or failure plan must be documented where another caregiver is responsible for material care.

- [ ] Client has provided instructions if the other caregiver does not arrive.
- [ ] Client/emergency contact notification path is clear.
- [ ] Cuddle Crew’s role is understood.
- [ ] Additional Service is subject to availability.
- [ ] Additional duties are not assumed automatically.

If another caregiver fails to show:

- Cuddle Crew does not silently assume unlimited extra responsibilities;
- contact the Client or emergency contact as appropriate;
- prioritize urgent welfare needs within current scope where reasonably necessary;
- determine whether additional Cuddle Crew Service is available;
- and document any change.

Additional care may require:

- an additional Service;
- changed scope;
- applicable pricing;
- or another approved arrangement.

## 17. Third-Party Schedule Change

If another caregiver:

- changes their visit time;
- cancels;
- adds a visit;
- removes a visit;
- or changes their responsibilities,

determine whether the overall care plan remains adequate.

- [ ] Client has been notified where appropriate.
- [ ] Cuddle Crew schedule remains workable.
- [ ] No medication or feeding conflict has been created.
- [ ] No care gap has been created.
- [ ] Revised responsibilities are clear.

Cuddle Crew is not automatically obligated to absorb another caregiver’s schedule change.

## 18. Changes Made by Third Parties

Cuddle Crew is not responsible for changes or problems caused by independent third parties outside Cuddle Crew’s reasonable control.

Potential third-party changes may include:

- moved supplies;
- changed feeding setup;
- altered medication organization;
- unlocked or locked doors;
- changed thermostats;
- changed alarm settings;
- modified pet confinement;
- removed equipment;
- changed litter/enclosure conditions;
- or other alterations.

If a third-party change creates a safety or care problem:

1. assess immediate welfare and safety;
2. do not improvise beyond approved scope;
3. contact the Client where appropriate;
4. document the condition;
5. modify only what can safely and reasonably be addressed.

## 19. Supplies and Equipment

- [ ] Shared caregivers know where appropriate supplies are located where necessary.
- [ ] Equipment needed by Cuddle Crew remains available.
- [ ] Other caregiver will not remove required equipment without coordination.
- [ ] Medication organization remains understandable and secure.
- [ ] Food portions/supplies remain sufficient.

If another caregiver’s actions cause necessary supplies to become unavailable, contact the Client and follow applicable replacement-supply or additional-trip procedures.

## 20. Behavior and Handling Consistency

Where behavior management is relevant:

- [ ] Material Client-approved handling rules are understood.
- [ ] Separation rules are clearly assigned.
- [ ] Predator/prey separation is reliable.
- [ ] Doors/gates/crates/rooms are used consistently where required.
- [ ] Another caregiver’s expected actions do not undermine the approved safety plan.

If another caregiver’s approach creates a material safety conflict, review the arrangement rather than assuming Cuddle Crew can compensate for it.

## 21. Shared-Care Medication Conflict

If medication records suggest that a dose may have been:

- duplicated;
- missed;
- administered at the wrong time;
- or administered differently from the approved plan,

do not independently guess whether another dose should be given.

Follow the approved medication and emergency plan.

Contact the Client, emergency contact, or veterinary resource as appropriate.

Document:

- what was observed;
- what information was available;
- who was contacted;
- and what instructions were received.

## 22. Responsibility Boundaries

Cuddle Crew is responsible for the Services Cuddle Crew has agreed to provide.

Cuddle Crew is not responsible for:

- supervising independent third parties;
- guaranteeing another caregiver’s attendance;
- guaranteeing another caregiver’s performance;
- correcting every third-party mistake;
- performing unlimited unscheduled replacement care;
- or assuming duties not included in the confirmed Service plan.

This does not prevent Cuddle Crew from taking reasonable action within scope when necessary to protect animal welfare during an active booking.

## 23. Pricing and Additional Care

Shared care itself does not automatically create a surcharge.

If another caregiver’s failure or a Client-requested change results in additional Cuddle Crew work, applicable pricing should be based on:

- additional booked Service;
- additional time;
- applicable short-notice treatment;
- applicable travel/access rules;
- or other CURRENT / APPROVED pricing provisions.

Do not invent a generic shared-care fee.

Do not charge Cuddle Crew’s Client for a third party’s mere existence.

## 24. Shared-Care Review Triggers

Use personalized review where shared care involves material complexity such as:

- unclear responsibility;
- conflicting schedules;
- medication shared between caregivers;
- exact-time medication;
- complex pet separation;
- predator/prey management;
- multiple outside caregivers;
- repeated access conflicts;
- unresolved emergency authority;
- unreliable third-party attendance;
- inadequate combined care frequency;
- or another arrangement where responsibility cannot be clearly established.

A review trigger is not an automatic decline.

Possible outcomes include:

- clarify responsibilities;
- modify schedule;
- modify Service;
- require additional documentation;
- require a signed Shared Care / Third-Party Care Agreement;
- personalize the care plan;
- refer;
- or decline.

## 26. Shared-Care Coordination Principle

A shared-care arrangement is acceptable only when responsibilities are clear enough that all involved parties can reasonably understand:

- who is providing each part of care;
- when that care is expected;
- who handles medication;
- how access works;
- how material changes are communicated;
- who has emergency responsibility;
- and what happens if another caregiver does not perform their assigned care.

The complete care plan must remain adequate for animal welfare.

An unidentified or unreliable person who **might** provide care is not a substitute for a defined care plan.

Cuddle Crew does not silently assume unlimited additional responsibilities when another caregiver fails to perform their assigned duties.

## Website Implementation Notes

The website may identify and support shared-care arrangements, but should not publish the full internal coordination checklist.

### Shared-Care Question

The inquiry or onboarding workflow may ask:

**Will anyone else be caring for your pets or accessing your home while Cuddle Crew is providing services?**

Examples may include:

- family;
- friends;
- neighbors;
- another pet sitter;
- dog walker;
- house sitter;
- or another service provider.

### Conditional Follow-Up

If the Client answers yes, the website or approved onboarding workflow may collect high-level information such as:

- caregiver/provider name;
- relationship/company;
- contact information where appropriate;
- dates/times involved;
- general responsibilities;
- whether they handle medication;
- whether they have home access;
- and whether they are part of the emergency plan.

Sensitive details should ultimately reside in Precise Petcare or another approved secure operational system.

### Responsibility Matrix

A secure onboarding workflow may allow the Client to assign responsibilities such as:

- morning feeding/potty;
- midday care;
- evening feeding/potty;
- medication;
- litter/enclosure care;
- home/security tasks;
- emergency response.

Do not make this information public.

### Uncertain Third-Party Care

If a Client indicates that someone:

- may stop by;
- might help;
- probably will visit;
- or has no defined schedule,

the system should not count that person as a guaranteed care visit when evaluating care frequency.

Where welfare depends on the uncertain care, route the plan to:

**Additional information needed**

or:

**Personalized review required**

### Medication

If multiple caregivers may administer medication:

- require clear responsibility assignment;
- do not auto-finalize an ambiguous plan;
- and route complex or exact-time arrangements to Medication Scope Review.

Do not automatically calculate dosing from shared-care responses.

### Access

The website may collect whether another caregiver will also access the home.

Do not expose:

- key locations;
- access codes;
- alarm credentials;
- lockbox combinations;
- or other security information

in public pages, analytics, URLs, or browser-visible configuration.

### Communication

Do not automatically require Cuddle Crew to communicate directly with every third party.

The Client remains the primary coordinator unless another arrangement has been approved.

### No-Show Logic

Where another caregiver is responsible for material care, the onboarding flow may ask:

**What should happen if this person cannot complete their scheduled care?**

The system must not automatically assume that Cuddle Crew will cover the missed care.

Appropriate client-facing language may state:

**Additional Cuddle Crew visits are subject to availability and applicable pricing.**

### Care-Frequency Logic

When evaluating whether the overall care plan is adequate:

- include only reliable, clearly assigned third-party care;
- do not count uncertain visits;
- do not allow a vague shared-care plan to reduce care below approved welfare standards.

### Pricing

Do not create a generic shared-care fee.

Additional charges must derive from CURRENT / APPROVED pricing rules for actual additional Cuddle Crew Services, time, travel, access, or other approved circumstances.

### Liability / Responsibility Messaging

The website may explain generally that:

**Cuddle Crew is responsible for the care Cuddle Crew agrees to provide and cannot control the acts or omissions of independent third parties.**

Do not expand or rewrite contractual liability language beyond the CURRENT / APPROVED Client-facing agreement.

### Public Status

Safe Client-facing statuses include:

- Information needed
- Shared-care details received
- Under review
- Personalized review required
- Care plan confirmed

Do not expose internal labels such as:

- unreliable caregiver;
- responsibility conflict;
- no-show risk;
- access conflict;
- or third-party risk score.

### Precise Petcare Handoff

Final shared-care information should be maintained in Precise Petcare, including:

- identified caregivers;
- assigned responsibilities;
- medication responsibility;
- access notes;
- communication plan;
- emergency hierarchy;
- and no-show plan.

The public website should not become a second authoritative shared-care record system.

### Privacy

Do not expose another caregiver’s:

- name;
- phone number;
- email;
- schedule;
- home-access details;
- medication role;
- or emergency role

publicly or in unnecessary analytics/logging.
