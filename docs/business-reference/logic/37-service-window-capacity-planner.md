## Purpose

This document defines the internal Service-window and capacity-planning logic used by Cuddle Crew Pet Care LLC.

Its purpose is to prevent scheduling decisions from being based only on the visible length of a Service or on apparently open calendar time.

Real appointment capacity must account for:

- booked hands-on care time;
- drive time before the appointment;
- drive time after the appointment;
- journal and administrative time;
- route positioning;
- realistic operational buffer;
- existing confirmed Services;
- pet-specific needs;
- and other schedule constraints.

This document is an internal operational reference.

It may inform website availability and booking-review systems, but the public website must not expose:

- private calendar details;
- other Client bookings;
- route order;
- drive-time calculations between Clients;
- personal/work/school schedule;
- internal buffers;
- maximum daily capacity;
- or private decline reasoning.

Precise Petcare should remain the operational source of truth for confirmed Client appointments and schedules.

## Authoritative Content

# SERVICE WINDOW / CAPACITY PLANNER

## 1. Standard Daytime Service Windows

Routine daytime appointments are generally scheduled within the following Service windows:

- **9:00 AM–12:00 PM**
- **12:00 PM–3:00 PM**
- **3:00 PM–6:00 PM**
- **6:00 PM–9:00 PM**

These are arrival windows, not exact-minute guarantees.

An exact-time requirement must be specifically reviewed and accepted.

## 2. Service Window Principle

Each standard daytime Service window contains approximately three hours, or 180 minutes.

However:

**180 minutes of calendar time does not equal 180 minutes of sellable pet-care time.**

Capacity must account for the complete operational load associated with each appointment.

## 3. Capacity Formula

Use the following internal formula:

**CARE TIME + DRIVE TIME + JOURNAL / ADMIN TIME + REALISTIC BUFFER = TRUE APPOINTMENT LOAD**

Where appropriate, drive time should include:

- travel into the appointment;
- travel from the appointment;
- or the portion of route travel reasonably attributable to the scheduling decision.

Do not evaluate capacity using hands-on care time alone.

## 4. Appointment Load Components

For each appointment, consider:

### Care Time

The full booked hands-on Service duration.

Examples:

- 30 minutes;
- 60 minutes;
- 90 minutes.

Booked duration belongs to the Client.

Ordinary travel between appointments must not be deducted from the Client’s booked care time.

### Drive Before

Expected travel time from:

- the prior Client;
- starting location;
- Overnight location;
- school/work/personal obligation;
- or other relevant route position

to the appointment.

### Journal / Administrative Time

Allow realistic time for applicable tasks such as:

- completing the Precise Petcare journal;
- uploading photos;
- reviewing Client notes;
- documenting medication;
- documenting unusual observations;
- updating the schedule;
- or other routine Service administration.

Documentation should not materially shorten promised Client care.

### Buffer

Allow reasonable unsold time for normal variability.

Buffer may account for:

- traffic variation;
- parking;
- apartment/building access;
- slower-than-usual pet care;
- Client communication;
- minor schedule delays;
- transitions;
- pet accidents;
- weather;
- or other ordinary operational variability.

A schedule that works only if every appointment, drive, and pet interaction goes perfectly is not a realistic schedule.

### Drive After

Consider travel required to reach:

- the next Client;
- another Service window;
- an Overnight;
- or another fixed obligation.

## 6. One 90-Minute Appointment

**General guidance: Often workable.**

One 90-minute appointment uses approximately half of a 3-hour window for direct care.

Approximately 90 minutes remain for:

- travel;
- journal/admin;
- buffer;
- and route positioning.

Do not assume the remaining 90 minutes can automatically accommodate another full Service.

## 7. Two 90-Minute Appointments

**General guidance: Generally not workable in one 3-hour window.**

Two 90-minute Services already consume all 180 minutes of the window before accounting for:

- any travel;
- documentation;
- parking;
- access;
- or buffer.

Do not schedule two standard 90-minute appointments in the same 3-hour window merely because:

`90 + 90 = 180`

The actual appointment load would exceed the available window once normal operations are included.

## 8. One 60-Minute + One 30-Minute Appointment

**General guidance: Often workable if geographically compatible.**

Combined hands-on care time:

**90 minutes**

This leaves approximately 90 minutes of the window for:

- travel between appointments;
- journal/admin;
- buffer;
- and route positioning.

Geographic compatibility still matters.

A distant 30-minute appointment may consume more capacity than a nearby 60-minute appointment once travel is included.

## 9. Two 60-Minute Appointments

**General guidance: Possible only with efficient routing and buffer.**

Combined hands-on care:

**120 minutes**

Approximately 60 minutes remain before accounting for:

- travel;
- documentation;
- parking/access;
- and buffer.

Two 60-minute appointments should not automatically be considered available merely because 120 minutes is less than 180.

Review the actual route.

## 10. Three 30-Minute Appointments

**General guidance: May fit with dense routing; do the math.**

Combined hands-on care:

**90 minutes**

This theoretically leaves approximately 90 minutes for:

- travel;
- documentation;
- access;
- and buffer.

Three 30-minute appointments may be workable where Clients are geographically close and access is efficient.

They may be unrealistic where:

- Clients are widely separated;
- parking is difficult;
- building entry is slow;
- one appointment has additional handling complexity;
- or documentation needs are unusually high.

Do not treat three 30-minute appointments as an automatic standard capacity.

## 11. Extended and Far Extended Clients

Travel minutes consume real capacity even when the travel surcharge makes the booking financially acceptable.

A Client in an Extended or Far Extended travel zone may have:

- an appropriate travel fee;
- but still be operationally difficult to fit into a Service window.

Pricing and capacity are separate decisions.

A surcharge may address economics.

It does not create additional minutes in the schedule.

## 12. Travel-Zone Interaction

Current daytime travel structure includes:

- Core: approximately 0–10 minutes typical one-way travel;
- Standard: more than 10–20 minutes;
- Extended: more than 20–30 minutes;
- Far Extended: more than 30–45 minutes;
- beyond 45 minutes: personalized review / generally unavailable unless specifically approved.

The existence of an approved travel zone does not guarantee capacity.

A geographically eligible Client may still be unavailable for a specific Service window because of route positioning.

## 13. Typical vs. Temporary Travel Conditions

Capacity planning should use realistic expected travel.

For established travel-zone classification, use typical or ordinary travel conditions rather than temporary traffic spikes.

For a specific day’s actual route planning, known conditions may still be considered operationally.

Do not confuse:

**travel-zone classification**

with:

**day-of route planning.**

A temporary traffic problem does not ordinarily reclassify the Client’s pricing zone.

It may still affect how much buffer or route adjustment is needed that day.

## 14. Route Position Matters

Appointment capacity depends on where the sitter is before and after the Service.

For example, the same 30-minute appointment may have very different capacity impact depending on whether it is:

- 5 minutes from the prior Client;
- 25 minutes from the prior Client;
- adjacent to the next Client;
- on the opposite side of the Service area;
- or between fixed obligations.

Do not evaluate appointments in isolation when route order matters.

## 15. Exact-Time Care

Exact-time requirements reduce scheduling flexibility and may consume more effective capacity than ordinary window-based Services.

If a Client requires exact-time care:

- [ ] exact-time requirement has been approved;
- [ ] route reliability has been reviewed;
- [ ] travel buffer is sufficient;
- [ ] adjacent appointments remain workable;
- [ ] the exact-time obligation does not create unreliable care elsewhere.

Exact-time care is a personalized-review trigger unless specifically approved.

Do not let a website form convert a routine window into an exact-minute commitment automatically.

## 16. Medication Timing

Medication timing may affect capacity where:

- exact timing is medically important;
- the allowed window is narrow;
- administration is time-intensive;
- administration reliability depends on pet cooperation;
- or failure/delay has significant consequences.

Use the Medication Scope Review where applicable.

Medication-related scheduling constraints must be considered as part of true appointment load.

## 17. Pet-Specific Care Complexity

A Service may require more operational flexibility where care includes:

- significant behavior management;
- medication;
- complex feeding;
- multiple enclosures;
- separation/rotation;
- slow senior care;
- difficult access;
- or another time-sensitive routine.

If the complete routine regularly approaches or exceeds the booked duration, reassess the Service plan rather than relying on schedule compression.

## 18. Arrival Windows

Arrival windows provide operational flexibility.

The standard windows allow Cuddle Crew to:

- organize route order;
- respond to normal pet-care variation;
- accommodate traffic;
- maintain realistic buffers;
- and avoid unnecessary exact-minute commitments.

Do not publicly convert Service windows into:

- exact arrival times;
- guaranteed first-hour arrival;
- exact route order;
- or narrower commitments

unless those commitments have actually been approved.

## 19. Client-Requested Preferred Time

A Client may express a preference within a Service window.

A preferred time is not automatically an exact-time commitment.

Internal scheduling may consider preferences where practical, but:

- animal welfare;
- medication timing;
- route efficiency;
- existing confirmed bookings;
- emergencies;
- and safety

may affect route order.

## 20. Journals and Documentation

Journal and administrative time are part of operational capacity.

Do not build a schedule that assumes documentation requires zero time.

Likewise:

- documentation should not materially shorten the Client’s booked care;
- travel should not be deducted from booked care;
- and journal time should not routinely be pushed indefinitely outside realistic working capacity merely to fit more Services.

## 21. Buffer Principle

A realistic buffer is required.

Do not schedule the day using a zero-buffer model.

Buffer should be sufficient to absorb normal variability without making later Client care unreliable.

The exact amount of buffer may vary based on:

- route;
- Service type;
- Client access;
- pet needs;
- traffic;
- and known complexity.

This document does not establish one universal fixed buffer for every appointment.

## 22. Calendar Availability vs. True Availability

A visually open slot does not automatically mean a booking can be accepted.

A slot may appear open while still being operationally unavailable because of:

- drive time;
- existing nearby bookings;
- route direction;
- journal/admin needs;
- an Overnight;
- fixed personal/work/school obligations;
- exact-time Services;
- or insufficient buffer.

Website availability must not treat blank calendar space as conclusive proof of capacity.

## 23. Overnights and Daytime Capacity

Overnight Care affects surrounding daytime scheduling.

When reviewing an Overnight, consider:

- evening travel to the Overnight location;
- morning travel away from the Overnight;
- separate midday care;
- other daytime Client appointments;
- personal/work/school obligations;
- and route sustainability.

An Overnight location that is operationally inconvenient may affect both the evening and following morning.

Use Overnight Acceptance and Long-Stay Review where applicable.

## 24. Holiday / Peak-Date Capacity

Holiday or peak-date pricing does not create additional capacity.

During high-demand periods:

- use the same true-load calculation;
- preserve realistic buffers;
- avoid compressing care;
- and decline additional work when the route is no longer reliably manageable.

A holiday surcharge addresses pricing.

It does not justify overbooking.

## 25. Short-Notice Requests

A short-notice or same-day fee does not create schedule capacity.

Before accepting:

- [ ] true appointment load calculated;
- [ ] route impact reviewed;
- [ ] existing confirmed care protected;
- [ ] appropriate buffer remains;
- [ ] Service can be completed reliably.

If not, decline or require personalized review.

## 26. Capacity Priority

Existing confirmed Client care should not be materially compromised to accept additional work.

Do not accept a new booking if doing so would reasonably require:

- shortening another Client’s booked Service;
- removing necessary travel time;
- eliminating realistic buffer;
- creating impossible arrival expectations;
- compromising medication timing;
- or otherwise making existing care unreliable.

## 27. Capacity Decision

For a proposed appointment or route, select one:

- [ ] **CAPACITY AVAILABLE**
- [ ] **CAPACITY AVAILABLE WITH ROUTE ADJUSTMENT**
- [ ] **CAPACITY AVAILABLE WITH CONDITIONS**
- [ ] **PERSONALIZED REVIEW REQUIRED**
- [ ] **ALTERNATE SERVICE WINDOW REQUIRED**
- [ ] **ALTERNATE DATE REQUIRED**
- [ ] **NO CAPACITY**

### Conditions / Notes

Record internally where relevant:

- preferred route order;
- appointment sequence;
- drive-time concern;
- buffer requirement;
- exact-time constraint;
- Overnight impact;
- Service-duration issue;
- or another operational consideration.

Do not expose another Client’s information in these notes unnecessarily.

## 28. Capacity Principle

Capacity is determined by the complete operational load, not by the number of visible appointments.

The governing formula is:

**CARE TIME + DRIVE TIME + JOURNAL / ADMIN TIME + REALISTIC BUFFER = TRUE APPOINTMENT LOAD**

The goal is not to maximize the number of appointments squeezed into each Service window.

The goal is to maintain a schedule that allows Cuddle Crew to provide:

- the full booked duration;
- reliable care;
- realistic travel;
- appropriate documentation;
- and enough flexibility to handle normal variability.

If a booking only fits by assuming perfect traffic, zero documentation time, no pet-care delays, and no buffer, the booking does not realistically fit.

**Request received**

**We’ll confirm availability after reviewing the route.**

- Client location;
- Service duration;
- travel estimates;
- existing confirmed appointments;

### Travel-Time Data

Do not expose:

- private route details;
- other Client addresses;

- or server-side routing credentials

through browser-visible configuration or public API responses.

### Existing Clients

Existing confirmed appointments take priority over new availability calculations.

Automation must not reshuffle or shorten confirmed Client care merely to create availability for another request.

### Exact-Time Logic

If a request includes exact-time care:

- do not automatically confirm from ordinary window availability;
- route to personalized review;
- preserve sufficient travel/buffer.

### Extended/Far Extended Clients

Do not assume a Client is schedulable merely because the applicable travel fee can be calculated.

Travel pricing and capacity remain separate.

### Estimate Builder

The estimate builder may calculate price without guaranteeing scheduling capacity.

Keep separate:

**Estimated price**

from:

**Availability / booking confirmation**

A price estimate does not reserve a Service window.

### Precise Petcare Handoff

Final confirmed:

- dates;
- Service windows;
- appointment durations;
- and Client-specific schedule

should remain in Precise Petcare.

The website should not become a competing authoritative appointment calendar unless an explicitly approved integration makes it part of the operational workflow.

### Internal Administration

An internal admin tool may display more detailed capacity information, including:

- Client appointment;
- care minutes;
- drive before;
- journal/admin;
- buffer;
- drive after;
- total load.

This information must remain authenticated and private.
