## Purpose

This document defines the internal continuity and backup-provider process used by Cuddle Crew Pet Care LLC when the primary owner-operator may be unable to begin, continue, or complete confirmed Client care.

Its purpose is to protect:

- animal welfare;
- Client communication;
- continuity of essential care;
- access/security;
- Client privacy;
- emergency handoff;
- accurate responsibility transfer;
- and appropriate refund or billing treatment.

This document is an internal operational reference.

It does not guarantee that a backup provider will always be available.

The public website may explain generally that Cuddle Crew maintains continuity procedures for unexpected emergencies, but it should not expose:

- backup-provider identities;
- private contact information;
- access-transfer methods;
- Client security information;
- medical information;
- internal availability;
- or private contingency details.

Precise Petcare should remain the operational source of truth for current Client-specific care instructions, access information, emergency contacts, schedules, and confirmed booking records.

- [ ] Emergency-contact pathway is current.
- [ ] Alternate transfer-of-responsibility plan is understood.

A Client’s decision to decline backup-provider access does not eliminate the need for an emergency continuity plan.

If Cuddle Crew cannot safely continue care and no authorized substitute may enter, responsibility may need to transfer back to:

- the Client;
- an emergency contact;
- another Client-authorized caregiver;
- or another appropriate person.

## 6. Scope Matching

Before assigning a backup provider to a specific Client, confirm:

- [ ] Species is within backup provider’s competence.
- [ ] Required medication is within backup provider’s competence/scope.
- [ ] Behavior needs are manageable.
- [ ] Access method is workable.
- [ ] Service type is appropriate.
- [ ] Overnight requirements are appropriate where applicable.
- [ ] Timing requirements are workable.
- [ ] Any exact-time care is specifically reviewed.
- [ ] Travel/location is workable.
- [ ] Provider is actually available.

An approved backup provider is not automatically approved for every booking.

## 7. Continuity Trigger

Use this plan when a confirmed Service is at risk because of circumstances such as:

- sudden illness;
- injury;
- hospitalization;
- family emergency;
- vehicle breakdown;
- unsafe travel conditions;
- unexpected incapacity;
- severe weather;
- evacuation;
- major property/access issue;
- or another circumstance that materially affects Cuddle Crew’s ability to provide confirmed care.

Do not activate a backup pathway merely for convenience where ordinary Service can still be provided reliably.

## 8. Step 1 — Assess Capacity and Immediate Risk

When continuity is threatened:

1. determine which upcoming Services are affected;
2. determine how soon care is due;
3. identify essential welfare needs;
4. determine whether the primary owner-operator can safely complete any portion of the care;
5. determine whether delay is acceptable;
6. determine whether a backup path is required.

- [ ] Affected bookings identified.
- [ ] Timing urgency identified.
- [ ] Medication urgency identified.
- [ ] Feeding/water/elimination needs identified.
- [ ] Immediate animal-welfare risk assessed.
- [ ] Primary provider capacity assessed.

The first question is:

**Can the upcoming care still be completed safely and reliably?**

If yes, ordinary Service may continue.

If no or uncertain, proceed with continuity planning.

## 9. Step 2 — Notify the Client

Contact the Client as soon as reasonably practicable when a confirmed Service is materially at risk.

Communication should explain, as appropriate:

- that an unexpected situation has occurred;
- which Service may be affected;
- what immediate care needs are being protected;
- whether an approved backup option is available;
- whether Client action is needed;
- and what the next update will be.

Do not provide unnecessary personal medical or private details about the owner-operator.

The Client needs operationally relevant information, not a complete explanation of the owner’s private circumstances.

## 10. Client Unreachable

If the Client cannot be reached:

1. use the approved emergency-contact hierarchy where appropriate;
2. determine whether an already authorized backup provider may be used;
3. prioritize immediate animal welfare;
4. remain within signed authority and Service scope;
5. document attempted contacts.

Do not give an unapproved person access solely because the Client is temporarily unreachable.

## 11. Step 3 — Use Only an Approved Backup Path

An emergency substitute may be used only where that person is:

- approved by Cuddle Crew;
- authorized under the applicable Client arrangement where required;
- appropriate for the specific care;
- and available.

Approved continuity paths may include:

- vetted Cuddle Crew backup provider;
- Client-authorized alternate caregiver;
- Client emergency contact;
- or another specifically authorized responsible person.

Do not substitute an unknown or unapproved person merely because they are convenient or nearby.

## 12. Backup Provider Acceptance

Before transferring care, confirm that the backup provider:

- [ ] Has accepted the assignment.
- [ ] Understands the required Services.
- [ ] Understands dates/times.
- [ ] Understands pet count/species.
- [ ] Understands medication requirements.
- [ ] Understands behavior/safety issues.
- [ ] Understands access requirements.
- [ ] Understands emergency contacts.
- [ ] Understands Client communication expectations.
- [ ] Is able to complete the assignment.

Do not transfer responsibility based on an unanswered message or tentative availability.

## 13. Step 4 — Protect Access and Privacy

Record:

**How keys/access may be transferred:** ____________________________________________

**How Client medical/security information may be shared:** ____________________________________________

Share only information reasonably necessary for the approved backup provider to complete the assigned care safely.

Potential necessary information may include:

- Service address;
- access method;
- pet names;
- care instructions;
- medication instructions;
- emergency contacts;
- relevant behavior/safety information;

## 14. Access Transfer

Where access must be transferred:

- [ ] Client authorization permits the transfer where required.
- [ ] Recipient is approved.
- [ ] Transfer method is secure.
- [ ] Only necessary credentials are provided.
- [ ] Transfer is documented.
- [ ] Return/deactivation plan is established.

Do not:

- leave keys in an insecure location;
- send access codes to an unapproved person;
- label keys with the Client’s full street address;
- or create unnecessary copies of Client credentials.

## 15. Digital Credentials

If a backup provider requires:

- lock code;
- alarm code;
- smart-lock access;
- building credential;
- or other digital access,

share it only through an approved secure method where reasonably possible.

Avoid exposing access credentials through:

- public website systems;
- public notes;
- unsecured URLs;
- analytics;
- or browser-visible frontend configuration.

## 16. Client Medical Information

Share only medical information reasonably necessary for the assigned care.

Examples may include:

- medication name;
- dose;
- timing;
- administration instructions;
- known relevant condition;
- veterinarian;
- emergency instructions.

Do not provide broader Client or pet medical records simply because they exist.

## 17. Step 5 — Protect Animal Welfare

Essential welfare takes priority during continuity disruption.

Prioritize, as applicable:

1. fresh water;
2. necessary feeding;
3. required medication;
4. elimination/potty needs;
5. safe containment;
6. temperature/environmental needs;
7. emergency veterinary care where authorized;
8. other essential welfare needs.

Nonessential tasks may be deferred if necessary to ensure that critical care is covered first.

## 18. Medication Priority

If medication is time-sensitive:

- [ ] Timing requirement identified.
- [ ] Backup provider is within scope for the medication.
- [ ] Instructions transferred accurately.
- [ ] Duplicate-dose risk prevented.
- [ ] Client/emergency contact notified where appropriate.

Do not assign medication to a backup provider whose competence or Service scope is insufficient.

If appropriate medication care cannot be provided through the approved continuity path, escalate to the Client, emergency contact, or veterinary resource as appropriate.

## 19. Behavior and Safety

Before transfer, communicate only relevant behavior/safety information necessary to protect:

- backup provider;
- pets;
- household;
- and third parties.

Examples may include:

- bite history;
- leash reactivity;
- escape risk;
- resource guarding;
- required separation;
- predator/prey risk;
- handling limitations.

Do not omit material safety information merely to preserve privacy.

Privacy and safety should both be protected through appropriate minimum-necessary disclosure.

## 20. Step 6 — Transfer Responsibility

When another person accepts care, document:

- who accepted responsibility;
- which pets;
- which Services;
- effective date/time;
- expected duration of handoff;
- access provided;
- key care instructions transferred;
- and Client notification status.

**Responsibility accepted by:** ____________________________________________

**Effective handoff date/time:** ____________________________________________

**Services transferred:** ____________________________________________

A handoff is not complete until responsibility has actually been accepted.

## 21. Partial Handoff

A backup provider may take responsibility for only part of a booking.

Examples:

- one visit;
- one day;
- medication only where appropriate;
- morning Services;
- Overnight only;
- or remainder of booking.

Where responsibility is divided:

- document exactly who is responsible for what;
- avoid overlapping assumptions;
- prevent duplicate feeding or medication;
- maintain clear Client communication.

Use Shared-Care Coordination logic where care will be divided among multiple providers.

## 22. Temporary vs. Remainder-of-Booking Transfer

Specify whether the handoff is:

- temporary;
- for one Service;
- for a defined period;
- or for the remainder of the booking.

Do not assume that a backup provider who handles one emergency Service has accepted the entire booking.

## 23. If Backup Is Unavailable

Record:

**If backup unavailable:** ____________________________________________

If no approved backup provider is available:

1. reassess whether the primary owner-operator can safely complete any essential care;
2. notify the Client as soon as possible;
3. contact the authorized emergency contact where appropriate;
4. determine whether a Client-authorized alternate caregiver can assume responsibility;
5. prioritize essential animal welfare;
6. arrange safe responsibility transfer;
7. document the outcome.

Do not use an unapproved caregiver solely because no approved backup is available.

## 24. Client Must Arrange Alternate Care

Where Cuddle Crew cannot provide or arrange continued Service within approved scope, the Client or authorized representative may need to arrange alternate care.

Communicate clearly:

- which Services remain at risk;
- when care is next required;
- what has already been completed;
- what Cuddle Crew can and cannot continue;
- and when responsibility must transfer.

Avoid ambiguous handoffs.

## 25. Active Animal-Welfare Emergency

If Cuddle Crew becomes unable to continue during an active care situation and immediate abandonment would create a material animal-welfare risk:

- take reasonable temporary action within current safety, legal, and Service scope;
- contact the Client/emergency contact;
- arrange safe transfer as soon as practicable;
- document responsibility transfer.

Termination or provider incapacity does not justify leaving an animal in immediate danger where reasonable safe action remains possible.

## 26. Step 7 — Refund / Adjustment

If a confirmed Service is not performed by Cuddle Crew or an approved substitute under the confirmed arrangement:

- refund or credit the unperformed Service as required by the applicable Client-facing agreement/policy;
- adjust the invoice where appropriate;
- document any replacement-care treatment.

Do not invent a new continuity fee.

Do not charge the Client twice for the same Service merely because responsibility transferred.

## 27. Backup Provider Compensation

If Cuddle Crew separately compensates an approved backup provider, handle that as an internal business arrangement unless the Client-facing booking terms expressly require another structure.

Do not expose internal provider compensation unnecessarily to the Client or public website.

Client-facing pricing should remain consistent with the applicable confirmed booking and current approved policies.

## 28. Refund Principle

The continuity plan itself does not create independent refund rules.

Refunds, credits, cancellation treatment, and unperformed-Service adjustments remain governed by:

- Master Service Agreement;
- Cancellation, Booking Change & Refund Policy;
- Pricing, Fees & Surcharge Policy;
- and the applicable confirmed booking.

If a CURRENT / APPROVED Client-facing policy conflicts with this internal process, the Client-facing policy controls the contractual subject.

## 29. Step 8 — Document

Create an internal continuity or incident record documenting:

- cause/category of continuity issue;
- affected Services;
- Client contacts;
- emergency-contact contacts;
- backup-provider contacts;
- responsibility transfer;
- access transfer;
- care completed;
- care not completed;
- medication status;
- refunds/adjustments;
- and follow-up needed.

Document facts rather than unnecessary personal or emotional commentary.

## 30. Post-Event Review

After continuity has been restored:

- [ ] Confirm Client understands final outcome.
- [ ] Confirm access has been returned/deactivated.
- [ ] Confirm keys are accounted for.
- [ ] Confirm backup provider no longer has unnecessary credentials.
- [ ] Confirm Precise Petcare records are accurate.
- [ ] Confirm invoice/refund adjustment is complete.
- [ ] Confirm medication documentation is complete.
- [ ] Confirm handoff responsibility ended appropriately.
- [ ] Identify process improvements.
- [ ] Update continuity plan if needed.

## 31. Credential Revocation

After backup access is no longer required:

- deactivate temporary codes where appropriate;
- recover physical keys where appropriate;
- remove unnecessary digital access;
- remove access to Client-specific information;
- and document completion.

Do not allow emergency access to become indefinite access by default.

## 32. Confidentiality

Backup providers must receive Client information only for legitimate continuity purposes.

Client information must not be used for:

- unrelated marketing;
- solicitation;
- independent Client poaching;
- personal purposes;
- or unrelated disclosure.

Any applicable confidentiality expectations should survive completion of the backup assignment.

## 33. Non-Solicitation / Client Relationship Boundaries

Where consistent with applicable business arrangements, backup providers should understand that emergency access to a Cuddle Crew Client does not automatically create an independent right to solicit or assume that Client relationship.

This internal expectation should not be used to restrict a Client from freely choosing another provider.

Client welfare and choice remain separate from internal provider-business arrangements.

## 34. No Guarantee of Backup Availability

Maintaining a continuity plan does not mean Cuddle Crew guarantees:

- that a backup provider is always available;
- that every Service can always be reassigned;
- that every medication/scope can be transferred;
- or that every emergency can be resolved without Client involvement.

Continuity planning reduces risk.

It does not eliminate all operational emergencies.

## 35. Public Continuity Claim

If the website discusses continuity, use careful language.

Appropriate concept:

**Cuddle Crew maintains contingency procedures for unexpected emergencies and will make reasonable efforts to protect your pet’s care and communicate promptly if a confirmed Service is at risk.**

Do not claim:

- guaranteed backup;
- guaranteed substitute;
- guaranteed uninterrupted care under every circumstance;
- or guaranteed emergency coverage

unless a future CURRENT / APPROVED Client-facing agreement expressly establishes that promise.

## 37. Continuity Principle

The continuity process follows this order:

1. **Assess capacity and immediate risk**
2. **Notify the Client**
3. **Use only an approved backup path**
4. **Protect access, privacy, and security**
5. **Protect essential animal welfare**
6. **Transfer responsibility clearly**
7. **Refund or adjust unperformed Services as required**
8. **Document the event and improve the plan**

The primary goal is safe, clear continuity of care.

The backup process must not create a second safety, privacy, or access problem while attempting to solve the first one.
